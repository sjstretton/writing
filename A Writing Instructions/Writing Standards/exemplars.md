# Exemplars

Writing worth imitating, grouped by the type of writing it models. For each piece, the note says what to copy. Read the original before imitating it: these notes are a guide to what to look for, not a substitute.

> These references were listed from memory by an agent and are well known, but check the details before citing any of them in a paper. Copyrighted texts are not stored in this repo. Add PDFs you hold legitimately to `A Writing Instructions/Claude + Scripts/Setup Claude/_reference/`.

## Concept papers: ideas that entered the discourse

These short pieces introduced a term that people still use. They are the models for the concept papers in `A Writing Instructions/PAPERS.md`.

- **Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (Harvard University Press, 1970).** Three plain words that now organize a whole field. *Copy:* name the concept with ordinary words, then show it at work in very different settings.
- **William Nordhaus, "Climate Clubs: Overcoming Free-Riding in International Climate Policy," *American Economic Review* 105(4), 2015.** A memorable name for a mechanism, with a model behind it. *Copy:* the title is the idea; the model supports a claim a policymaker can repeat.
- **George Akerlof, "The Market for 'Lemons'," *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 84(3), 1970.** A vivid example (used cars) carries a general result. *Copy:* lead with the concrete case, then generalize.
- **Martin Weitzman, "Prices vs. Quantities," *Review of Economic Studies* 41(4), 1974.** A sharp question in the title and a clear answer: which instrument is better depends on the relative slopes of marginal costs and benefits. *Copy:* a title that is the question; a result that fits in one sentence.

## Working papers and academic writing

- **Ronald Coase, "The Problem of Social Cost," *Journal of Law and Economics* 3, 1960.** Argument by worked example (the rancher and the farmer), with almost no mathematics. *Copy:* make the reader see the mechanism through cases.
- **Paul Krugman, "Ricardo's Difficult Idea" (1996 essay).** Explains comparative advantage, and why intelligent people resist it, to non-economists. *Copy:* anticipate the reader's misunderstanding and address it directly.
- **Dario Amodei and coauthors, "Concrete Problems in AI Safety" (arXiv, 2016).** Turned a diffuse worry into five named, tractable research problems. *Copy:* the model for proposal 3. Name the problems; make each one researchable.

## Policy writing

- **IMF, *Fiscal Monitor: How to Mitigate Climate Change* (October 2019).** A finance-ministry audience, with a clear recommendation and the numbers behind it. *Copy:* the executive summary; claims first, numbers attached.
- **World Bank, *State and Trends of Carbon Pricing* (annual).** The house style for carbon pricing. *Copy:* the key-messages page; each message is one claim with one number.
- **Stéphane Hallegatte and coauthors, *Shock Waves: Managing the Impacts of Climate Change on Poverty* (World Bank, 2016).** A flagship that keeps a single argument across a long report. *Copy:* the overview chapter's first-sentence discipline.
- **Nicholas Stern, *The Economics of Climate Change: The Stern Review* (2006), Summary of Conclusions.** Confident, quotable conclusions for a political audience. *Copy:* how a technical result becomes a sentence a minister repeats.

## Popular philosophy and essays

The goal here is accessibility without loss of rigor.

- **Peter Singer, "Famine, Affluence, and Morality," *Philosophy & Public Affairs* 1(3), 1972.** One example (the drowning child) carries a demanding argument. *Copy:* a single vivid case that the reader cannot shake off.
- **Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?," *Philosophical Review* 83(4), 1974.** A title that is the problem; plain prose about a hard question. *Copy:* make the abstract tangible with one image.
- **Bertrand Russell, *The Problems of Philosophy* (1912).** Short chapters, everyday examples (a table), no jargon. *Copy:* start from what the reader already believes.
- **Harry Frankfurt, *On Bullshit* (essay 1986; book 2005).** A short, funny, rigorous analysis of an everyday concept. *Copy:* take a familiar word seriously and define it precisely.
- **George Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" (1946).** Both a model and a set of rules.
