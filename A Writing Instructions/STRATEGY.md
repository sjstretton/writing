## Strategic specialisms

What Stephen can credibly claim to be expert in, and the angle that makes him distinctive. *(Agent draft. Confirm, cut or reorder.)*

### In general

| Specialism | Distinctive angle |
|---|---|
| Carbon pricing design | Treats carbon pricing as a problem of tax base and trade, not just price level: destination-based pricing, feebates and the "race to the top". |
| Climate finance and the cost of capital | Argues that the cost of capital, not the cost of technology, is what holds back clean power in developing countries, and that it is several distinct risks, each with its own fix. |
| Sovereign incentives | Asks what would make a *government* choose differently, and designs rewards (guarantees, notional balances) rather than project-by-project subsidies. |
| Policy modelling | Builds models a finance ministry can audit: low-parameter CES demand, policy-equivalent carbon prices, AI-assisted recoding of CPAT. |
| Institutions and philosophy | Game theory, institutional economics and philosophy (Western and Indian) applied to how durable collective arrangements emerge. Master's degrees in philosophy, economics and physics. |
| Systemic AI safety *(emerging)* | Brings institutional and incentive design to AI risk at the level of systems, not single models. To be developed in proposal 3. |

## Positioning in one paragraph

*(Draft for the website "About" page, applications and bios. Confirm before use.)*

Stephen Stretton is an economist at the World Bank who works on how finance ministries and development banks can make climate policy work in practice. His research covers carbon pricing that survives open economies, the cost of capital for clean power in developing countries, and incentives that change what sovereign governments choose to do. He builds tools that ministries can audit and use, and brings training in philosophy, economics and physics to questions of institutional design, including, increasingly, the systemic risks of AI.
