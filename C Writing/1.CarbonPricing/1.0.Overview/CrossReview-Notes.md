# Cross-review notes for the carbon-pricing series

Issues found while drafting, to resolve in the cross-review pass across all briefs and working papers.

1. **Electricity across the two tracks (from the 1.2 brief agent).** 1.1 puts generation on the production track (intensity metric, feebate; detail in 1.2) but also lists "power sold to the grid" under the consumption track's forward obligation. Resolution: generation is priced by the feebate (production track); electricity sold to final users bears the consumption obligation on its embodied carbon, net of carbon prices paid upstream (1.4 brief already says this). Fix the wording in the 1.1 brief and working paper.
2. **Series titles.** Update the CFPB entries in every brief and working paper .bib to the final titles: 1.2 "Feebates: The Full Carbon Price at the Margin, Without the Carbon Bill"; 1.4 "Carbon Consumption Tax: A Carbon Component in VAT and a Forward Obligation on Energy Suppliers"; 1.3 and 6.1 when final.
3. **1.4 brief refers to "the working paper" (Stephen's earlier v0.1).** Rephrase so the brief stands alone.
4. **Feebate abatement relative to a tax.** IMF Fiscal Monitor (Oct 2019) puts feebate packages at roughly 50–70 percent of a tax's emission cuts (per the 1.2 brief agent; verify). Cite in 1.1 Section 5 alongside Proposition 4.
5. **French bonus-malus.** It cost €285m in 2008 and, per D'Haultfœuille et al., raised net emissions: a lesson on benchmark updating. Make sure no paper calls it costless.
6. **1.1 brief Proposition numbering** (1–5) differs from the 1.1 working paper (1–7). Fine, but cross-references between papers must name the result, not the number.
7. **CBAM recognition in 1.1 (from the 1.3 brief agent).** Default carbon prices sit in Article 9(4) of Regulation 2023/956 as replaced by Regulation 2025/2083; the implementing act under 9(5) governs conversion only. 9(4) allows a default only "where a carbon price was set by the rules applicable in the third country" and nets rebates. Recognizing feebates, subsidy reform or non-price instruments therefore needs a broad reading of "compensation" or an amendment. Soften 1.1 (brief and working paper, Section 6 / race section) to match, and clear the VERIFY on `EU2023` (Article 9 is right, as replaced by 2025/2083; add a bib entry for 2025/2083).
8. **Scrap shuffling fix.** 1.1 credits "benchmark design"; 1.3 makes country-sector measurement of the price signal and non-punitive defaults the main fix, with product benchmarks and scrap opportunity cost as labelled extensions. Align 1.1's wording.
9. **Currency.** 1.1 uses dollars, 1.3 euros for CBAM charges. Acceptable if each paper is internally consistent; state the currency in tables.
10. **Data provenance.** The 1.3 agent did not use numbers from the World Bank CBAM dashboard or the Egypt guides, treating them as internal. The EU default values used in 1.1 come from the Commission's public file (Implementing Regulation 2025/2621), which is fine. Do not publish the dashboard or Egypt workbook numbers.
11. **TECP formula correction (from the 6.1 brief agent; checked).** All source versions of 6.1 (qmd, tex, archive, workbook) multiply the switching semi-elasticity by an extra (1−w). The exact first-order CES result is ε̃_int = σ(e/p − κ), ε̃_ext = |η|κ, with κ the cost-share-weighted emissions per dollar of energy spending: d ln q_f = −σ(d ln p_f − Σ_g w_g d ln p_g) already contains the own-share effect. With the extra factor a uniform tax no longer conserves energy within a sector. The brief uses the corrected form (example: $33.2 first order vs $31.7 with the old form; full nonlinear $36.9). The working paper and the workbook need the same fix. **Flag to Stephen.**
12. **The qmd's Appendix A.7 + A.9 double-count (e − ē)** when combining $/GJ policy prices with semi-elasticities. Fix in the 6.1 working paper.
13. **"CBAM switching-margin price equals the feebate rate"** holds only when routes make the same product (steel); not in general. Check 1.1 and 1.3 wording.
14. **Prior art for the carbon-price equivalent (from the 6.1 literature agent).** Black, Minnett, Parry, Roaf & Zhunussova (IMF WP 22/254, 2022) define an economy-wide "carbon price equivalent" computed with CPAT for the G20; Parry, Black & Roaf (2021) propose effectiveness-weighting. 1.1 (brief and working paper) and 1.3 use "carbon-price equivalent" as if new: cite Black et al. 2022 and attribute the concept; 6.1 claims only its specific contributions.
15. **Private cost vs emissions effect.** Equal TECP does not mean equal private cost (regulations cost firms less than a tax at the same shadow price). Matters for CBAM recognition in 1.1 and 1.3: recognition by TECP credits emissions effect, not cost. Say so.
16. **EU power "moved to output-based allocation"** is wrong (power fully auctioned since 2013). Check that no paper says it.
