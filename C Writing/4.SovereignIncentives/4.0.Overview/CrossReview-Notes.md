Every `CFPB2x`, `CFPB3x` and `StrettonWP2x`/`3x` entry in all ten bibs now uses the final title from each paper's YAML, including "Reward the Stock as Well as the Flow: Notional Capitalized Value and Guarantee-Based Climate Incentives" and "From Projects to Asset Classes: Two-Stage Finance and Synthetic Sovereign Bonds for Clean Power". 3. **Wrong book subtitle in 3.2.** Both 3.2 bibs called the book manuscript *Time and Chance: Risk, Guarantees and the Cost of Clean Power in Developing Countries*. They now use the subtitle in the book master (`6.Additional/6.Books/6.1.TimeAndChance/TimeAndChance.qmd`): *The Elements of Scale in the Green Transition*. See open item 3.
4. **US spelling in the text.** Fixed: "Standardised" (3.0 title in the 3.2 brief's related list), "modelled", "centres", "cheque", "labelled" (4.1, 4.3), "channelling" and "rechannelling" (4.2), "cancelling" (4.1), "towards" (3.1) and "instalments" (3.1). 5. **Guarantee loss parameters (4.3 against 4.1 and 4.2).** 4.3 used a 1 percent annual default probability, 40 percent loss given default and a 10 percent buffer costing 2 percent a year, which gave a ratio of about 5. 4.1 and 4.2 use 3 percent annual calls, correlation 0.2, 37 percent loss per call (Cruces and Trebesch 2013) and a 9.3 percent buffer held in safe assets, which gives 2.7 (10 at a 10 percent loss). **4.3 is now aligned.** `2.Source/model.py` takes the series base case as named constants and prints the sensitivities. Both figures were redrawn: Figure 2 now plots the call probability from 0.5 to 5 percent at a 37 percent loss and marks the base case. The text is updated in the abstract, contribution (4), Table 3 and its note, Section 5.2, the Figure 2 caption, Section 7.1, Section 8, Appendix B (the threshold is now 189 bp, not 260) and Appendix C, and in the brief's executive summary, Figure 1 caption and Section 6.
   - New 4.3 numbers: \$3.3 billion of capacity is worth \$772 million to the government and costs the funder \$286 million in expected loss (was \$154 million), with a \$310 million buffer held. The ratio is 2.7 (was 5.0), 10 at a 10 percent loss per call, and 2.3 with a 2 percent buffer carry. At 300 bp the leverage runs from about 16 (0.5 percent calls) to 1.6 (5 percent). At 150 bp it falls below 1 above about 4 percent.
   - 4.1's abstract, conclusion and brief abstract now say "about 2.7 dollars", not "about three", so all three papers quote the same multiplier: 2.7, 10 at a 10 percent loss, about 80 on the MDB record.
   - The 2.1 ratio of 5.3 (tariff saving per dollar of expected cost for a contract guarantee on utility arrears) is a different quantity, on different parameters. It is not inconsistent with the sovereign base case, but readers comparing the series should be told so (open item 6).

## Decisions applied, 29 October 2026 (later the same day)

Stephen decided three open items. They supersede items 5 and 10 above where they differ.

1. **Series 4 headline is now the MDB loss record (open item 2).** The base case in 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3 is the multilateral development banks' sovereign loss record (GEMs 2025: sovereign and sovereign-guaranteed lending 1984–2024, default rate 0.77 percent a year, recovery 95.1 percent): call probability 0.77 percent, loss per call 4.9 percent, expected loss 0.038 percent a year. Correlation 0.2 across 20 programs and the 1 percent annual exhaustion target are unchanged and stated. The buffer is 0.5 percent of exposure (two calls), so \$5 billion supports about \$1 trillion on paper. The incentive per donor dollar is about 80 (63 with a 2 percent carry). The former base case (3 percent calls, 37 percent loss, 9.3 percent buffer, \$54 billion per \$5 billion, ratio 2.7) is now the **stress case**, and 3 percent calls with a 10 percent loss (2.5 percent buffer, ratio 10) is the **middle case**. Every paper says the record reflects preferred-creditor status that a climate guarantee facility might not inherit, which is why the stress case is reported, and that a facility can price on the record but hold the stress-case buffer until it has its own record.
   - Models rerun: `4.1…/2.Source/model.py`, `4.2…/2.Source/model.py`, `4.3…/2.Source/model.py`, each with named cases. Figures redrawn: 4.1 Figure 2 (log scales, three cases marked); 4.2 Figures 1–3 and the brief figure; 4.3 Figures 1–2.
