# Peer review of the section 5 overview, v0.2, and its theme deck (31 Oct 2026)

Written before revision, after papers 5.1–5.4 were peer-reviewed and revised; it checks the overview against them.

## Summary judgement

A tight overview with the right through-line: a model a ministry can use must be small enough to state, built twice and tested. Its numbers are those of the v0.1 papers and several are now wrong; it has no citations.

## Major points

1. **Equations and cost of capital (5.1).** "About eighteen equations" is fourteen. The default costs of capital of 7.5/10/12.5/15 percent by income group are an option in CPAT; the default is a country rate plus a technology premium. The logit's real flaw, per 5.1, is that listing a technology twice changes the shares (solar listed twice rises from 36 to 58 percent of new investment under the velocity rule).
2. **Bug counts (5.2).** 228 legacy bugs (24 high priority) at release 1.0.2; the register lists 231 at release 1.1.0. Say which.
3. **Validation (5.3).** Add the numbers: back-cast level error 5 percent of mean emissions for Australia and 92 percent for China; a \$75 tax cuts G20 CO₂ by 29 percent in 2030 by default, 7–39 percent as elasticities move; on a common growth-rate basis CPAT's response to a \$10 price, about 0.7–0.8 percent a year, is above the most comparable ex-post study's 0.5 percent (author's calculation).
4. **Elasticities (5.4).** "Seven of ten; three too high" is now eight of ten within the published range once CPAT's own calibration targets are removed from the evidence, two too high (diesel in industry, gasoline in services) and one at the edge; road gasoline (−0.64) is twice the bias-corrected estimate (−0.31); applying the long-run response in year one overstates five-year responses by 45–72 percent.
5. **Sources.** "More than 180 countries" and the "57,000-word documentation" should be cited (the CPAT Documentation's foreword says "more than 180 countries"); give the overview a bibliography. Note the corrected CPAT-AI documentation edition of 31 October 2026.
