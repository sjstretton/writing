# Fuel price shocks from subsidy reform (emerging economies) and the 2022 European gas shock as natural experiments for carbon pricing

Research notes for the working paper "What carbon prices did: ten natural experiments". Compiled 1 November 2026. Ex post evidence only. Where a source's counterfactual comes from a model, that is flagged. Carbon-price equivalents (CPE) are my own calculations from the cited price changes; the assumptions are stated in Key Question 3 and every CPE should be read as approximate.

**Access legend:** [FT] = I read the full text (the version is noted). [ABS] = abstract or bibliographic record only. [SNIP] = search-engine summary only; I did not open the page. Treat [SNIP] numbers as provisional.

**Recommendation in one line:** Document fully (1) **Germany 2022 gas** (Ruhnau et al. 2023, *Nature Energy*), the strongest ex post evidence; (2) **Indonesia 2013–2015** (electricity: Burke and Kurniawati 2018; road transport: Burke, Batsuuri and Yudhistira 2017), peer-reviewed panel econometrics; and (3) **Saudi Arabia 2016 and 2018** (Aldubyan and Gasim 2021; Gasim et al. 2023), peer-reviewed, but the counterfactual comes from an estimated demand model. Iran (2010, 2019), Indonesia 2005, Nigeria 2023 and Mexico 2017 have only administrative or press before-and-after figures; summarise them briefly.

## 1. Which candidates have credible ex post quantitative estimates?

### Takeaway
Three cases have peer-reviewed ex post estimates that quantify the demand response and allow a CO2 figure: Germany 2022 (gas), Indonesia 2013–2015 (electricity and road transport) and Saudi Arabia 2016/2018 (gasoline and residential electricity). For Iran, Nigeria, Mexico and Indonesia 2005, I found only before-and-after volumes from officials or the press, confounded by rationing, smuggling and hoarding. For Malaysia, India, Egypt, Ecuador and Ghana, I found no ex post estimate within this session's search budget.

### Cited Findings

**Tier 1: peer-reviewed, ex post, fuel use quantified against a counterfactual**
- **Germany 2022 gas.** Ruhnau, Stiewe, Muessel and Hirth estimate monthly gas savings for small consumers, industry and power separately. They control for "the non-linear temperature-heating relationship, seasonality, and trends". They find that "gas consumption during the second half of 2022 was 23% below the temperature-adjusted baseline" (abstract). [FT, accepted manuscript] — [Ruhnau et al. 2023, PIK open-access manuscript](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf); bibliographic data confirmed at [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natene/v8y2023i6d10.1038_s41560-023-01260-5.html)
- **Indonesia, electricity 2013–2015.** "subsidy reductions since 2013 had induced savings in annual electricity use of around 7% relative to the no-reform counterfactual as of 2015" (abstract). The data are a three-dimensional panel (six consumer groups, 16 regions, 1992–2015) with fixed effects and an IV. [FT, ANU working paper version, January 2018] — [Burke and Kurniawati 2018, WP 2018/01](https://crawford.anu.edu.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/2018-01_burke_and_kurniawati_wp_0.pdf)
- **Indonesia, road transport 2013–2014.** The 2013 and 2014 fuel subsidy reforms "reduced traffic pressure on these roads in the second half of 2015 by around 10% relative to the counterfactual without reform" (19 toll roads, monthly, 2008–2015) (abstract). [FT, ANU WP 2017/10; journal abstract checked] — [Burke, Batsuuri and Yudhistira, WP 2017/10](https://crawford.anu.edu.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/2017-10_burke_ea_wp_0.pdf); [journal abstract, IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transa/v105y2017icp167-180.html)
- **Saudi Arabia 2018.** The authors "econometrically model gasoline and residential electricity demand using Harvey's (1990) Structural Time Series Model". Their decomposition "reveals that these energy price reforms were the largest contributors to the observed decreases in gasoline and residential electricity consumption in 2018" (journal abstract). [ABS for the journal version; FT for the KAPSARC discussion-paper version, June 2020] — [Aldubyan and Gasim 2021, abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/enepol/v148y2021ipbs0301421520306522.html); [KAPSARC DP KS--2020-DP12 full text](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Saudi Arabia 2016–2018, economy-wide.** The authors "estimate the cumulative carbon dioxide emissions avoided between 2016 and 2018 due to energy price reform at 164 million tonnes" (five end-use sectors, 15 energy products) (abstract). [ABS] — [Gasim et al. 2023, Energy Economics](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eneeco/v120y2023ics0140988323000877.html)

**Tier 2: credible ex post working papers that complement Tier 1 (flagged as not peer-reviewed or not about price alone)**
- **Germany, descriptive.** Moll, Schularick and Zachmann (Brookings Papers on Economic Activity, Fall 2023) find that "the German economy reduced gas consumption by about 20%, driven mostly by industry (26%) and households (17%)" for July 2022–March 2023 against the 2019–21 average. This comparison is **not** temperature-adjusted (Table 2 notes). [FT, BPEA conference draft, 26 September 2023] — [Moll, Schularick and Zachmann 2023](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/6_Moll-et-al_unembargoed_updated.pdf)
- **Europe, cross-country decomposition.** The EBRD paper decomposes the 2022–23 winter fall in gas use across 25 European economies plus Norway. "Warm temperatures ... account for 4 pp of the average reduction ... weak industrial activity accounts for another 2.7 pp, increased use of renewables (and coal) in electricity generation for 1.1 pp. The unexplained remainder, of around 6 pp, is equivalent to heating buildings by around 1.3 degrees less" (abstract). EBRD Working Paper No. 284, prepared December 2023. [FT] — [Plekhanov and Sassoon 2023](https://ebrd.com/content/dam/ebrd_dxp/assets/pdfs/office-of-the-chief-economist/working-papers/working-papers-2023/WP-284-Gas-consumption-in-Europe-during-the-winter-of-2022-23-v3b-1-2.pdf)

### Inferences
- The paper can claim three cases with credible quantified responses: German gas, Indonesian electricity and road travel, and Saudi gasoline and electricity. In all three the response is a **short-run, inelastic** reduction in the range of roughly 6–28%, against price increases of roughly 25–580%.
- Only Germany has a counterfactual built **without** an elasticity assumption (a regression baseline with temperature controls). Indonesia and Saudi Arabia build the counterfactual by applying estimated elasticities to the observed price path. They are "ex post econometric", but the counterfactual itself is model-derived. Say so in the paper.

### Gaps
- No search was run for Malaysia (2014 RON95 float), India (2014 diesel deregulation), Egypt (2014–2019), Ecuador or Ghana, because the search budget went on the Tier 1 cases. I cannot say whether credible ex post estimates exist for them.
- I did not consult IMF (2013) "Energy Subsidy Reform: Lessons and Implications", the IISD GSI country reviews, or the KAPSARC gasoline studies (e.g., Atalla, Gasim and Hunt 2018), which might have more ex post Indonesian, Iranian or Saudi data.
- I found no peer-reviewed ex post econometric study of gasoline or diesel *volumes* for Indonesia's 2005, 2008 or 2014/15 reforms. Burke et al. measure toll-road trips, not fuel.

## 2. Price path: retail prices before and after each step, by fuel and sector

### Takeaway
Germany: wholesale gas went from €15–20/MWh to €100–200/MWh. Industrial purchase prices peaked at 580% of pre-crisis levels (Sept 2022) and household prices at +130% (Nov 2022), against much smaller semi-annual average increases in Eurostat data. Indonesia: gasoline (Premium RON 88) went 4,500 → 6,500 → 8,500 IDR/l over June 2013–Nov 2014 (+89% cumulative), then down to about 6,600–7,600 in early 2015. Saudi Arabia: 91-octane gasoline went 0.45 → 0.75 → 1.37 SAR/l (2016, 2018), and the first residential electricity block went 0.05 → 0.18 SAR/kWh (2018).

### Cited Findings

**Germany 2021–2023, natural gas (all sectors; industry and small consumers analysed separately)**
- Spot prices reached "100–200 €/MWh in 2022 ... about ten times the long-term pre-Covid price levels of 15–20 €/MWh". They spiked to 227 €/MWh (invasion, 24 Feb 2022) and 339 €/MWh (Nord Stream 1 closure announced 19 Aug 2022) (p. 1). — [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- "the average gas price paid by German industry has increased six-fold when it peaked in September 2022, somewhat lagged and dampened by long-term contracts" (p. 1). In the price-response discussion: "As of September 2022, average gas prices for industrial consumers peaked at 580% of pre-crisis levels (January 2019 to September 2021)" (p. 5). — [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- Households: "Average German residential retail prices increased more than two-fold between January and November 2022, before a political intervention substantially reduced households' energy bills in December 2022" (p. 1). "For small consumers, prices have increased by 130% between 2019-2021 and their peak in November 2022" (p. 4). The price series are Destatis indices (Figure 1 caption). — [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- Small consumers reacted late partly because they "typically have retail contracts with fixed prices over longer time spans such as a year ... most consumers in this segment were not exposed to rising wholesale prices" (p. 3). — [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- Eurostat semi-annual average prices for Germany, in EUR/kWh (retrieved 1 Nov 2026):
  - Households, band D2 (20–199 GJ/yr), all taxes included: 2019-S1 0.0632; 2019-S2 0.0588; 2020-S1 0.0597; 2020-S2 0.0620; 2021-S1 0.0647; 2021-S2 0.0692; 2022-S1 0.0806; 2022-S2 0.0941; 2023-S1 0.1230; 2023-S2 0.1145. — [Eurostat nrg_pc_202 API query](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/nrg_pc_202?format=JSON&lang=EN&geo=DE&nrg_cons=GJ20-199&unit=KWH&tax=I_TAX&currency=EUR&sinceTimePeriod=2019-S1)
  - Non-households, band I4 (10,000–99,999 GJ/yr), excluding VAT: 2019-S1 0.0318; 2019-S2 0.0300; 2020-S1 0.0289; 2020-S2 0.0289; 2021-S1 0.0319; 2021-S2 0.0379; 2022-S1 0.0533; 2022-S2 0.0613; 2023-S1 0.0808; 2023-S2 0.0742. — [Eurostat nrg_pc_203 API query](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/nrg_pc_203?format=JSON&lang=EN&geo=DE&nrg_cons=GJ10000-99999&unit=KWH&tax=X_VAT&currency=EUR&sinceTimePeriod=2019-S1)
- Relief measures that diluted the retail price signal:
  - VAT on gas cut "from 19% to 7%", which mutes "the price-signal for consumers" (p. 24). — [Moll et al. 2023](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/6_Moll-et-al_unembargoed_updated.pdf)
  - The "gas price brake" credits households with "80% of their historical consumption times the difference between the current market price [and] 12 cents per kWh". The marginal price "remains equal to the pre-intervention market price", so it works as a lump-sum transfer, not a price cap (pp. 24–25). — [Moll et al. 2023](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/6_Moll-et-al_unembargoed_updated.pdf)

## 3. Carbon-price equivalent (US$ per tonne CO2)

### Takeaway
On a per-step basis, the subsidy-reform shocks equal implicit carbon prices of roughly **US$35–145/tCO2** for gasoline and **US$40–125/tCO2** for diesel. The German 2022 gas shock equals roughly **€400–900/tCO2** at the marginal (wholesale or peak-retail) price, or **€150–300/tCO2** on Eurostat semi-annual average retail prices. Measured by CPE, these are some of the largest "carbon prices" ever observed, and the demand responses are modest.

### Method and assumptions (my calculations; state them in the paper)
- CPE = (price increase per unit in local currency ÷ exchange rate) ÷ CO2 per unit.
- Emission factors:
  - gasoline 2.3 kg CO2/l (the brief's figure, also used by Aldubyan and Gasim, KAPSARC DP p. 17);
  - diesel 2.68 kg/l (brief);
  - kerosene 2.52 kg/l (my assumption: IPCC default 71.9 tCO2/TJ × about 35 MJ/l) [VERIFY];
  - natural gas 0.202 tCO2/MWh on a net calorific basis (IPCC 56.1 tCO2/TJ). German retail prices are quoted per kWh on a gross calorific (Hs) basis, where the factor is about 0.182 t/MWh, which raises the CPE by about 10% [VERIFY conversion];
  - Indonesian grid electricity about 0.81 kg/kWh (implied by Burke and Kurniawati's 13 Mt for 16 TWh);
  - Saudi electricity 0.6 kg/kWh (authors' assumption, DP p. 17).
- Exchange rates:
  - IDR: monthly averages from FRED series CCUSMA02IDM618N (OECD MEI). Mar 2005 9,372.70; Oct 2005 10,080.76; May 2008 9,272.64; Jun 2013 9,877.80; Nov 2014 12,158.30; Jul 2017 13,342.10 IDR/US$. — [FRED CCUSMA02IDM618N](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CCUSMA02IDM618N)
  - EUR: 2022 average of FRED EXUSEU monthly values = 1.0538 US$/€ (Sep 2022 0.9899; Nov 2022 1.0192). — [FRED EXUSEU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/EXUSEU)
  - SAR: pegged at 3.75/US$ (consistent with the DP's own US$ conversions in Table 1). — [KAPSARC DP Table 1](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Relative to observed carbon prices, the gas shock is extreme.** A wholesale-equivalent CPE of about €400–900/t is 5–10 times typical EU ETS prices, and it brought about a 23% temperature-adjusted fall in gas use over a half-year. That points to an implied short-run elasticity of about −0.04 (industry) to −0.16/−0.27 (small consumers), as Ruhnau et al. compute.
- **The subsidy-reform CPEs (about US$35–145/t per step) are in the range of today's higher carbon prices.** Their measured responses are about 6–10% within one to two years, consistent with elasticities of −0.1 to −0.4.
- **Iran's CPE in US$ terms depends heavily on which exchange rate is used.** Report both rates, or express Iran's CPE as a share of GDP per capita or in PPP terms.

## 4. Ex post estimates: method, data, period, effect on fuel use and CO2, elasticities

### Takeaway
- **Germany:** gas use fell 23% below a temperature-adjusted baseline in H2 2022. Industry went from −4% (Sept 2021) to −27% (Oct 2022); small consumers peaked at −28% (Sept 2022). The implied elasticities are small (−0.04 industry, −0.16 to −0.27 small consumers).
- **Indonesia:** electricity use was −7% (16 TWh, about 13 Mt CO2/yr) by 2015, with elasticities of −0.15 to −0.2 same-year and −0.4 over four years. Toll-road trips were −10% by H2 2015 (elasticity −0.1 immediate, −0.2 at 12 months).
- **Saudi Arabia:** gasoline −2.0 bn l (short run) / −2.8 bn l (long run) in 2018, or 4.6/6.4 Mt CO2; residential electricity −9 TWh (139 → 130 TWh), about 5.6 Mt CO2; cumulative 164 Mt CO2 across all products over 2016–2018 (model-based).

### Cited Findings

#### Germany 2021–2022 (Ruhnau et al. 2023) [FT, accepted manuscript]
- **Method.**
  - Three separate OLS regressions of monthly gas consumption (small consumers; industry; power) on monthly crisis dummies, month-of-year dummies, a linear trend and (for small consumers and industry) a simulated heating-demand profile.   - Standard errors are "heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation robust". Prices are deliberately excluded "to avoid problems with endogeneity and measurement error" (p. 3).

- **Method:** Harvey's Structural Time Series Model of per-capita gasoline and residential electricity demand, with a stochastic underlying energy demand trend (UEDT). Annual data. An LMDI decomposition splits the observed 2018 change into price, income, population, UEDT, weather and residual effects. — [KAPSARC DP, Methods](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Elasticities:** "long-run price and income elasticities for gasoline of -0.13 and 0.15, and for residential electricity of -0.09 and 0.22". — [KAPSARC DP, Summary](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Observed vs attributed:** "In 2018, residential electricity usage fell by 13.0 terawatthours (TWh) and gasoline consumption by 2.2 billion liters versus 2017. Higher residential electricity and gasoline prices contributed 9.1 TWh and 1.9 billion liters". — [KAPSARC DP, Key Points](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Counterfactual and CO2:**
  - Gasoline: the 2018 reform "saved between 2.0 billion liters in the short run and 2.8 billion liters in the long run compared with the no-reform scenario ... reduced the country's carbon footprint by 4.6 million tonnes annually in the short run and 6.4 million tonnes annually in the long run".
  - Electricity: "Residential electricity consumption drops from 139 TWh under a no-reform scenario to actual consumption of 130 TWh ... around 5.6 million tonnes of avoided CO2 emissions in that year" (pp. 15–17).

  — [KAPSARC DP](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Welfare:** gains "of 8.8 and 3.8 billion SAR annually (2.3 and 1.0 billion US$)" (journal abstract). — [Aldubyan and Gasim 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/enepol/v148y2021ipbs0301421520306522.html)
- **Context:** annual gasoline consumption "quadrupled from 8 billion to 32 billion liters" between 1983 and 2018. — [KAPSARC DP, Introduction](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Economy-wide (Gasim et al. 2023):** STSM across five sectors and 15 products. Demand is "price inelastic in all cases". Avoided CO2 is "164 million tonnes" cumulatively over 2016–2018; welfare gain 11.6 billion (2016) and 17.0 billion (2018) 2010 US$. [ABS] — [Gasim et al. 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eneeco/v120y2023ics0140988323000877.html)

### Inferences
- **Gross CO2 from German gas savings (my calc.):** the Moll et al. 157 TWh (Jul 2022–Mar 2023, not temperature-adjusted) is about 29–32 Mt CO2 (0.182–0.202 t/MWh): industry about 18–20 Mt, households about 11–12 Mt. Ruhnau's peak month (25 TWh, Nov 2022) is about 5 Mt CO2. This is **gross**; see Q5 for substitution.
- **Indonesia Oct 2005, illustrative only:** the Pertamina m/m drop is about 86,000 t CO2/day (gasoline 15,000 kl × 2.3; diesel 14,200 kl × 2.68; kerosene 5,500 kl × 2.52), or about 2.6 Mt a month if sustained. The September base is probably inflated by pre-announcement hoarding (see Q5), so this overstates the effect.
- **Saudi relative effects (my calc.):** residential electricity −9/139 ≈ −6.5% against no reform. Gasoline −2.0 bn l against roughly 34 bn l without reform ≈ −6% in the short run.
- **Common pattern:** price increases equivalent to US$35–150/tCO2 (subsidy reforms) produce 6–10% demand reductions within one to two years. Very large shocks (German gas, €400+/t) produce 20–28%. The response is roughly proportional to the log price change with elasticities of −0.1 to −0.4, consistent with the meta-analytic literature cited in these papers.

- Ruhnau et al.: Supplementary Table 2 (monthly point estimates with CIs, in TWh) was not seen. The H2 2022 absolute savings in TWh are not stated in the main text. The manuscript's Methods mention "14 dummy variables for the crisis period from September 2021 until October 2022", while the results cover to December 2022. [VERIFY against the published version.]
- No ex post estimate of German gas savings converted to CO2 *by the authors* was found.

## 5. Confounders and threats to interpretation

### Takeaway
- **Germany:** the "crisis effect" bundles prices with public appeals, ethical motives and information campaigns. Industrial savings include output cuts and import substitution (carbon leakage). Power-sector gas savings were offset by more coal, so net national CO2 fell only about 1.9% in 2022.
- **Subsidy reforms:** pre-announcement hoarding (Indonesia 2005), rationing and quotas (Iran 2007, 2010, 2019), smuggling (Iran, Nigeria), cash transfers (Iran, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia), macro shocks (2008–09 GFC; Iran 2019 protests then COVID-19; Nigeria's 2023 devaluation) and price reversals (Indonesia late 2014, 2016).

### Cited Findings

**Germany 2022**
- **Price vs other drivers:** "we estimate the effect of the crisis event and cannot disentangle the causal effect of prices from other potential drivers such as public attention". The March 2022 drop in residential use "cannot well be explained by steadily increasing household prices", and "may be driven by increased attention ... and ethical concerns". The government launched an energy-saving information campaign in June 2022 (pp. 2–5). — [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- **Industry output and pass-through:** "industrial natural gas consumption may not only be affected by price changes but by other external factors such as economic activity. Indeed, economic activity was relatively high in many crisis months ... This suggests that the actual price response was even larger" (p. 5). — [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- **Coal-for-gas switching in power:** "As early as by May 2021, natural gas prices were high enough to make gas-fired power plants the most expensive generators, i.e., inducing fuel switching toward coal plants". Power-sector gas use was also driven by renewables output, the "administrative phase-out of coal and nuclear plants", low French nuclear availability and the southern European drought (p. 4). — [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- **Net national emissions:** German GHG emissions in 2022 were about 746 Mt CO2e, "gut 15 Millionen Tonnen weniger als 2021" (−1.9%). Energy-sector emissions rose by about 10.7 Mt to about 256 Mt because of more hard coal and lignite in power generation. Industry emissions fell 10.4% to 164 Mt, reflecting sharply lower energy inputs "insbesondere in der metallverarbeitenden und chemischen Industrie". UBA forecast, March 2023; may since have been revised. [SNIP] — [UBA press release](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/uba-prognose-treibhausgasemissionen-sanken-2022-um); [Bundesregierung](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/schwerpunkte/klimaschutz/emissionsdaten-2022-2171208)
- **Import substitution (leakage):** "BASF drastically reduced the production of ammonia ... then switched to producing ammonia in its other plants around the world, including in the U.S. ... and more generally to importing ammonia". The paper shows "substantial increases in net imports of energy-intensive products" (rubber tyres, plastics, aluminium) (pp. 21–22). — [Moll et al. 2023](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/6_Moll-et-al_unembargoed_updated.pdf)
- **Gas-to-oil switching in EU industry:** an IEA estimate of "around 7 bcm of gas-to-oil switching" in industry in 2022, with production in gas-intensive sectors down about 8%, "less than the corresponding reduction in their gas consumption". [SNIP; VERIFY in the IEA commentary or the "Gas Market Lessons from the 2022-2023 Energy Crisis" report.] A Columbia CGEP analysis instead argues the industrial drop came mainly from lower output, not switching. [SNIP] — [IEA commentary](https://www.iea.org/commentaries/europe-s-energy-crisis-what-factors-drove-the-record-fall-in-natural-gas-demand-in-2022); [Columbia CGEP](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/publications/anatomy-of-the-european-industrial-gas-demand-drop)
- **EU coal in power:** small. EBRD attributes only 0.2 pp of the average gas reduction to "switching electricity generation to other fossil fuels such as coal" (p. 3). — [Plekhanov and Sassoon 2023](https://ebrd.com/content/dam/ebrd_dxp/assets/pdfs/office-of-the-chief-economist/working-papers/working-papers-2023/WP-284-Gas-consumption-in-Europe-during-the-winter-of-2022-23-v3b-1-2.pdf)
- **Weather:** "Warm temperatures during the winter of 2022-23 are estimated to account for 4 pp of the average reduction". Comparisons that do not adjust for temperature (e.g., Eurostat's −17.7%, Moll et al.'s −20%) therefore overstate behavioural savings. Ruhnau et al. stress that year-on-year comparisons "cannot distinguish between savings in response to the crisis and the effect of stochastic weather variations" (p. 2). — [Plekhanov and Sassoon 2023](https://ebrd.com/content/dam/ebrd_dxp/assets/pdfs/office-of-the-chief-economist/working-papers/working-papers-2023/WP-284-Gas-consumption-in-Europe-during-the-winter-of-2022-23-v3b-1-2.pdf); [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)
- **Relief measures:** the VAT cut from 19% to 7% muted the price signal. The gas price brake preserved the marginal price (Moll et al., pp. 24–25). Some industrial consumers received government support, reducing their exposure, while "others may be able to resell their contracted gas volumes at the spot market" (Ruhnau et al., p. 5). — [Moll et al. 2023](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/6_Moll-et-al_unembargoed_updated.pdf); [Ruhnau et al. 2023](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28398_3/component/file_28572/28398oa.pdf)

**Indonesia**

Toll-tariff changes are controlled for, though their elasticity is hard to identify (fn 14). — [Burke et al. WP 2017/10](https://crawford.anu.edu.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/2017-10_burke_ea_wp_0.pdf)
- **Price reversal:** subsidised fuel prices "were lowered in early 2016 due to the falling world oil price, likely partially reversing the effect" (p. 16). — [Burke et al. WP 2017/10](https://crawford.anu.edu.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/2017-10_burke_ea_wp_0.pdf)
- **Electricity:**
  - Captive (own) generation is excluded from the data; prepaid revenue is mismeasured regionally.
  - Industry had sector shocks: −7% (1998 AFC) and −11% (2009 GFC), controlled with dummies (Table 2).
  - On substitution: "both (a) oil use by industry, and (b) total energy use by industry were lower in 2015 than 2013. There thus does not appear to have been large substitution to other energy sources" (fn 10, p. 25).
  - Poor households were exempt from the increases (pp. 6, 8).

  — [Burke and Kurniawati WP](https://crawford.anu.edu.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/2018-01_burke_and_kurniawati_wp_0.pdf)

**Saudi Arabia**
- **Cash transfers:** the Citizens' Account Program was launched with the 2018 wave (income effect). — [KAPSARC DP](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Efficiency trend:** residential energy-efficiency measures cut electricity use "by at least 7.7 TWh" between 2014 and 2018. This is separated from price in the decomposition. For gasoline, "exogenous factors (such as urban sprawl, congestion, and road network expansion) may have obscured any such gains". — [KAPSARC DP, Summary](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)
- **Few substitutes:** the authors point to "the absence of alternatives to personal vehicles for transportation in major cities". — [KAPSARC DP, Summary](https://cdn.kapsarc.org/media/5izl45wg/energy-price-reform-in-saudi-arabia-modeling-the-economic-and-environmental-impact-and-understanding-the-demand-response.pdf)

**Iran**
- **Rationing since 2007:** "The rationing of gasoline started in June 2007 reduced demand growth and smuggling". The reform was timed for "the period of the year with the lowest energy use, around November-December". Unused old quota at 1,000 rials "effectively delayed and moderated the price increase for many smaller gasoline users". — [Guillaume et al. 2011](https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-full-text-pdf/external/pubs/ft/wp/2011/_wp11167.pdf)
- **Smuggling:** it is "unclear how much of the consumption cut was due to a reduction in smuggling". [SNIP] — [IAEE newsletter](https://iaee.org/en/publications/newsletterdl.aspx?id=197)
- **2019 bundling:** the 2019 price rise came with a new quota (60 l/month), so the price effect cannot be separated from the quota effect. CNG use rose. Then came nationwide protests and an internet shutdown (Nov 2019), US sanctions, and COVID-19 from February 2020. [SNIP; COVID timing is general knowledge, VERIFY with a source] — [Peak Oil News summary](https://news.peak-oil.org/2019/11/irans-gasoline-consumption-plunges-after-massive-price-hike/)

**Nigeria**
- **Supply, not consumption:** NMDPRA "consumption" is truck-out (supply). Before removal it is widely believed to have included cross-border smuggling, so part of the post-removal "drop" is less smuggling and diversion, not lower use. Figures conflict by source and month. [SNIP] — [BudgIT](https://budgit.org/subsidy-removal-and-nigerias-daily-petroleum-consumption/); [ThisDay, Sept 2024](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/09/10/despite-scarcity-nigerias-daily-petrol-consumption-surges-to-50m-litres-amid-smuggling-concerns/)

### Inferences
- **Germany's net CO2 effect is much smaller than the gross gas saving.** About 29–32 Mt of gross gas-related CO2 was avoided over Jul 2022–Mar 2023 (Q4 inference). Against that, power-sector emissions rose about 10.7 Mt in 2022 (coal) and some industrial gas switched to oil. Net national GHG fell only about 15 Mt (−1.9%) in 2022. Part of the industrial saving is leakage (imports of ammonia and other gas-intensive goods), which does not cut global emissions. For the paper: a broad, uniform carbon price would treat coal more harshly than gas. A gas-only price shock is not a carbon price, and it can raise emissions in the power sector.
- **For subsidy reforms, the CPE overstates the "carbon price" seen by consumers who received offsetting cash transfers (Iran, Saudi Arabia).** It also overstates it where quotas kept infra-marginal litres cheap (Iran). Only the marginal price matters for substitution, as the German gas brake design shows.
- **Smuggling makes measured domestic-volume falls an upper bound on the true consumption response** (Iran, Nigeria, possibly Indonesia).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate was found of the share of German or EU gas savings offset by oil or coal, in tonnes of CO2. The IEA's "7 bcm gas-to-oil" figure needs verification from the primary IEA source, which returned 403.
- I did not fetch UBA's final (non-forecast) 2022 inventory figures.
- No source was found on fuel smuggling volumes around Indonesia's 2005, 2013 or 2014 reforms.
