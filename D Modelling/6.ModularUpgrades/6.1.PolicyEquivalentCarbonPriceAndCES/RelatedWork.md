# Related work

Companion C3 of the series led by 1.1: the Total Effective Carbon Price, the metric that expresses any policy as a carbon-price equivalent, used in 1.1 for policy crediting and border recognition. Moved from `4.Modelling/4.1` on 29 Oct 2026 and numbered 6.1 in section 6 (modular upgrades) on 31 Oct 2026; the qmd is the master (v0.4, 31 Oct 2026: peer-reviewed in `5.Notes/PeerReview-2026-10-31.md`, references re-checked in `5.Notes/BibCheck-2026-10-31.md`, executive summary added). The policy brief is a separate master, `1.Presentation/Brief-PolicyEquivalentCarbonPriceAndCES.qmd` (v1.1), with a ten-slide deck built from `1.Presentation/slides.json`. Earlier versions (working paper v0.2 and v0.3, brief v1.0, old deck) are in `8.PreviousVersions/`.

- `8.PreviousVersions/`: v0.1 is the earlier pair of working papers (Paper 1 TECP, Paper 2 CES and partial adjustment), later merged into the master. `TECP-2026-09-shortLaTeXvariant.tex` is a later, shorter LaTeX variant (1,900 words), kept for reuse, not a master.
- `9.Archive/`: executive summary, formulae and CES conversion notes; `TECP_Prototype.xlsx`.
- `2.Source/`: the v0.3 scripts (`tecp_model.py`, `verify_derivations.py`, `make_wp_figures.py`) and figures; the model workbook (superseded: v0.2 semi-elasticities) and old docx/tex renders.
- The CPAT architecture paper (`5.Modelling/5.2.CPATAIImplementation`) applies the same CES demand structure. Paper 5.4 (`5.Modelling/5.4.ElasticityValidation`) runs this paper's model with CPAT's demand elasticities (TECP of the example mix \$33.1 against \$36.9); 6.1 v0.4 reports this in its calibration section.
