## Q1. Policy design: coverage, rate path, freeze, later increases, elimination, revenue recycling

### Takeaway
The tax started on 1 July 2008 at C$10/t CO2e and rose C$5 each 1 July to C$30/t on 1 July 2012. It was then frozen until 1 April 2018. After that it went to C$35 (2018), C$40 (2019, held through March 2021), C$45 (2021), C$50 (2022), C$65 (2023) and C$80 (2024), and was set to zero from 1 April 2025. The tax covered roughly 70–75% of provincial GHG emissions. It was legislated as revenue neutral and, through 2012/13, returned more than it raised, mainly via personal and corporate income-tax cuts plus the Low Income Climate Action Tax Credit. From Budget 2018 the new revenue was explicitly earmarked for relief, industry and green programs.

### Cited Findings
**Design and coverage**
- The 2008 Budget backgrounder says the tax "will apply to virtually all fossil fuels, including gasoline, diesel, natural gas, coal, propane, and home heating fuel". It "starts at a rate based on $10 per tonne … and will rise by $5 a year for the next four years — reaching $30 per tonne by 2012." **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2008 Backgrounder](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2008/backgrounders/2008_Backgrounder_Carbon_Tax.pdf)
- At announcement the schedule was "2.41 cents per litre for gasoline, rising gradually to 7.24 cents a litre by 2012", and "2.76 cents per litre [diesel and home heating oil], rising to 8.27 cents". These were **projections**; the enacted per-litre rates were lower (see Q2). **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2008 Backgrounder](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2008/backgrounders/2008_Backgrounder_Carbon_Tax.pdf)
- Coverage is "about three-quarters of all greenhouse gas emissions in the province" by 2012. **[abstract]** — [Murray & Rivers 2015, Energy Policy 86](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/enepol/v86y2015icp674-683.html)
- Pretis says coverage is "roughly 70% of greenhouse gas emissions (Murray and Rivers 2015) with comparatively few exemptions". Footnote 1 lists the main exemptions: "fuel exported from the taxed region, fugitive emissions, fuel used by shipping and air-travel to or from the region, and greenhouse operations in agriculture". Industrial process (non-combustion) emissions are also outside the base (p. 116 and the sectoral section). **[full text]** — [Pretis 2022, ERE 83:115–144](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10640-022-00679-w)
- The 2013 Carbon Tax Review froze the rate: "Carbon tax rates will not be increased. They will be maintained at $30 per tonne of CO2e" (Budget and Fiscal Plan 2013/14–2015/16, pp. 59–60). It added a carbon-tax relief grant for commercial greenhouse growers "set at 80 per cent of the carbon tax paid on specified fuels" (p. 60). **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2013 BFP](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2013/bfp/2013_Budget_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)
- The biofuel adjustment, per Rivers and Schaufele: "When the carbon tax was first introduced, the projected per litre tax at $30tCO2e equalled 7.24 cents per litre. Starting in 2010, the per litre carbon taxes were revised downward to reflect the province's biofuel mandate. Biofuels such as ethanol and biodiesel are exempt from the tax." (footnote 6). **[WP full text]** — [Rivers & Schaufele 2012, uOttawa WP 1211E](https://ruor.uottawa.ca/bitstreams/71b10df6-81e0-456f-b649-76fe2e78cb98/download)
- A search summary says that effective 1 January 2010 the gasoline and light fuel oil rates were each cut by 5%. **[snippet]** [VERIFY: primary BC notice not retrieved] — [Ryan summary of BC Budget 2010](https://ryan.com/canada/about-ryan/news-and-insights/2010/march/british-columbia-budget-2010/)

**Rate path ($/t CO2e)**
- 2008–2012: C$10 (1 July 2008), +C$5 each 1 July, C$30 on 1 July 2012. The footnote to Table 1 of the Revenue Neutral Carbon Tax Report reads "starting at $10 per tonne effective July 1, 2008 and increasing by $5 per tonne each July 1 to $30 per tonne on July 1, 2012" (p. 61). **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2013 BFP](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2013/bfp/2013_Budget_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)
- Budget 2018, Table 1 "Carbon Tax Rates – 2018 to 2022" (p. 75): "Prior to 2018 … $30; April 1, 2018 … $35; April 1, 2019 … $40; April 1, 2020 … $45; April 1, 2021 … $50; April 1, 2022 … $50". This was the *plan*. The 2020 step was later deferred, which pushed C$45 to 2021 and C$50 to 2022 (see the official past-rates table in Q2). **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2018 BFP](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2018/bfp/2018_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)
- The official BC "past carbon tax rates" page lists periods "April 1, 2019 to March 31, 2021" (gasoline 8.89 ¢/L = C$40), "April 1, 2021 to March 31, 2022" (9.96 ¢/L = C$45), "April 1, 2022 to March 31, 2023" (11.05 ¢/L = C$50) and "April 1, 2023 to March 31, 2024" (14.31 ¢/L = C$65). **[full text]** — [BC Gov, Past carbon tax rates](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/motor-fuel-carbon-tax/publications/carbon-tax-rates-by-fuel-type/past-carbon-tax-rates)
- For 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025 the rates are gasoline 17.61 ¢/L, light fuel oil (incl. diesel) 20.74 ¢/L, natural gas $3.9859/GJ or 15.25 ¢/m³, propane 12.38 ¢/L. "Carbon tax was eliminated effective April 1, 2025." **[full text]** — [BC Gov, Carbon tax rates by fuel type](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/motor-fuel-carbon-tax/publications/carbon-tax-rates-by-fuel-type)
- Carbon Tax Act, Schedule 1: Column 3 is "Rate of tax for the year starting April 1, 2024" (Gasoline 17.61 ¢/L; Light Fuel Oil 20.74 ¢/L; Natural Gas 15.25 ¢/m3). Column 4 is "Rate of tax for the year starting April 1, 2025 and each subsequent year": 0.00 for every fuel. Natural-gas retailers must refund any tax collected on gas delivered on or after 1 April 2025 (s. 36(1.1)). **[full text]** — [Carbon Tax Act, SBC 2008 c. 40 (consolidated)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/08040_01)
- The C$80 figure for 2024 is not printed on the official rate pages. I infer it from 17.61 ¢/L ÷ ~0.22 kg/L, and Wikipedia also states it [secondary]. Wikipedia gives the 2025–26 revenue loss from repeal as C$1.99 billion **[secondary]**. — [Wikipedia, BC carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_carbon_tax)

**Revenue recycling**
- 2008 design: bottom two PIT rates cut "2 per cent in 2008 and 5 per cent in 2009 on the first $70,000"; general CIT rate 12%→11% from 1 July 2008, with a plan for 10% by 2011; small-business rate 4.5%→3.5% from 1 July 2008, with a plan for 2.5% by 2011; and a new Climate Action Credit of "$100 per adult and $30 per child per year". Revenue was forecast at "$1,849 million over three years". There was also a separate one-time C$100 "Climate Action Dividend" per resident. **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2008 Backgrounder](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2008/backgrounders/2008_Backgrounder_Carbon_Tax.pdf)
- Revenue Neutral Carbon Tax Report, Table 1 (Budget 2013, p. 61). 2011/12: carbon tax revenue C$959m against designated tax reductions C$1,141m. 2012/13 (revised forecast): C$1,172m against C$1,375m. Items: BC Low Income Climate Action Tax Credit (184)/(195); 5% cut in the first two PIT rates (220)/(235); Northern and Rural Homeowner Benefit (66)/(67); general CIT cut to 10% (381)/(450); small-business CIT cut to 2.5% (220)/(261). Reductions exceeded revenue by C$182m in 2011/12 and C$203m in 2012/13 (p. 62). **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2013 BFP](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2013/bfp/2013_Budget_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)
- Budget 2018 ("A New Approach to Climate Action and Carbon Pricing", p. 75) says new revenue from rate increases will "fund the transition to a sustainable, low-carbon province". It names three uses: relief for low- and moderate-income households, support for emissions-intensive industry (a CleanBC-style incentive refunding up to 100% of tax "beyond $30 per tonne"), and new green initiatives. The Low Income Climate Action Tax Credit maximum rises "by $19.50 to $135 per adult from the current $115.50" and "to $40 per child from the current $34.50". This ends strict revenue neutrality for the increments above C$30. **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2018 BFP](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2018/bfp/2018_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)
- Budget 2018 projected carbon tax revenue of C$1,248m (2017/18), C$1,488m, C$1,725m and C$1,974m (the following years), PDF p. 137. **[full text]** — [BC Budget 2018 BFP](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2018/bfp/2018_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)
- Murray and Rivers find the tax "largely met revenue neutrality" early on (wording from secondary summary). The abstract says effects on income distribution are "relatively small". **[abstract]** — [Murray & Rivers 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/enepol/v86y2015icp674-683.html)

### Inferences
- For a natural-experiment table, three regimes are cleanest: **Phase 1** (July 2008 – July 2012, C$10→30, revenue neutral, the period nearly all ex post studies use); **freeze** (July 2012 – March 2018); **Phase 2** (April 2018 – March 2025, C$35→80, not revenue neutral, overlapping the federal backstop era). Most peer-reviewed ex post estimates cover Phase 1 data only, ending 2011–2016.
- Over 2008–2012 the recycling was skewed to business tax cuts (C$829m of C$1,375m in 2012/13). This matters for interpreting the GDP and employment results (Q6).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the 1 April 2020 deferral announcement. The deferral is inferred from the official period "April 1, 2019 to March 31, 2021" at the C$40 rate. [VERIFY: BC government COVID-19 measures, March 2020]
- I did not confirm whether large industrial emitters moved to the BC Output-Based Pricing System from 1 April 2024 or 2025. This matters for whether "elimination" was consumer-only. [VERIFY: BC OBPS start date and coverage]

## Q2. Per-unit rates on gasoline, diesel and natural gas, and their share of the retail price

### Takeaway
At C$30/t the tax was 6.67 ¢/L on gasoline, 7.67 ¢/L on diesel and $1.4898/GJ on natural gas. In July 2012 that was about **4.8% of the Vancouver self-serve regular pump price** (138.2 ¢/L) and about **5.8% of diesel** (133.2 ¢/L). At C$80 (April 2024) it was 17.61 ¢/L, about **8.4% of a 210.2 ¢/L Vancouver price**. On residential gas the C$30 tax was roughly 15% of a typical bill, because BC commodity gas was cheap. Introduced in July 2008 at the oil-price peak, the first step (2.34 ¢/L) was only ~1.6% of the pump price.

### Inferences
- The carbon tax's share of the pump price stayed small until 2023: under 5% of the gasoline price through 2012 and 5–6.5% to 2022. Pump-price swings in the same window were far larger: Vancouver regular fell 38.5 ¢/L (−26%) between July 2008 and July 2009, while the tax rose 1.17 ¢/L. This is why salience/identification arguments dominate the gasoline literature.
- Vancouver prices include TransLink's regional fuel tax. So the carbon-tax share of price in the rest of BC is slightly higher than the Vancouver-based shares above; Victoria July 2012 gives 6.67/123.9 = 5.4%.
- Pretis (p. 130) writes that the 2018 C$5 increase took the tax "from 6.7 to 8.9 cents per litre". The official rates are 6.67 → 7.78 (C$35); 8.89 is the C$40 (2019) rate. Do not reuse that sentence.

- The primary BC rate notices for 2008, 2010 and 2011 (diesel and natural gas) were not retrieved; Wayback Machine access was blocked.

## Q3. Ex post evidence: gasoline (household and road transport)

### Takeaway
Every credible gasoline study finds that the carbon tax cut BC gasoline demand by more than an equal market-price change would. The salience ratio ranges from about 2.9 (household microdata) to 4–7 (provincial panel). Published headline effects: **Lawley and Thivierge, a 5 ¢/L tax → −8% household gasoline** (lower bound −5%, semi-elasticity −0.016 per ¢/L). **Rivers and Schaufele, 2.4 Mt CO2 avoided** in the first four years (journal abstract; earlier versions said 3.5–3.6 Mt). **Antweiler and Gulati (WP), fuel demand 7% higher without the tax.** A 2022 synthetic-control study (Arcila and Baker) dissents.

### Cited Findings
**Rivers and Schaufele (2015)**
- Rivers, N. & Schaufele, B. (2015). "Salience of carbon taxes in the gasoline market." *Journal of Environmental Economics and Management* 74: 23–36. ## Q4. Ex post evidence: diesel (commercial and freight)

### Takeaway
Diesel demand responded to the carbon-tax component but not to other price components (salience). The total effect was small. ## Q7. Implied elasticities, salience, and caveats

### Takeaway
BC is the textbook case for **tax salience**: measured responses to the carbon-tax component are 2.9× (household microdata) to 4–7× (provincial panel) the response to equal market-price moves. But the mechanism is disputed (expectations of permanence, rational habits). The main identification threats are oil-price and recession swings in 2008–09, cross-border fuelling (strong C$ 2010–14), the concurrent biofuel mandate and transit investments, the 2012–18 freeze, and the small donor pool of 9 provinces.

### Cited Findings
- Salience ratios: 2.9 (L&T, per [CRDCN](https://crdcn.ca/publication/refining-the-evidence-british-columbias-carbon-tax-and-household-gasoline-consumption/)); 4.9 (R&S 2012 WP) and 7.1 (R&S 2014 manuscript) ([WP](https://ruor.uottawa.ca/bitstreams/71b10df6-81e0-456f-b649-76fe2e78cb98/download), [2014](https://uwo.scholaris.ca/items/b5e97440-77e2-48bc-9a7d-d5ffae3735c3)); 4.1 in the published R&S version [VERIFY, snippet]. For diesel, the carbon-tax term is significant and other price components are not ([B&K WP](https://ruor.uottawa.ca/bitstreams/5b3840ba-a78f-4bff-a863-b8111c893ebe/download)).
- Alternative mechanism: rational habits / perceived permanence ([Scott 2014](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:3bb78b31-17cc-4cbf-9887-744f314f2865)). The amplified response appeared "only initially", possibly because the tax was constant after July 2012 ([Erutku & Hildebrand 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cpp/issued/v44y2018i2p126-133.html)). Pretis's synthetic control shows a transport "re-bound" after the 2012 freeze ([Pretis 2022, p. 134](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10640-022-00679-w)).
- Cross-border fuelling: L&T adjust for it (lower bound −5%) ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/enejou/v39y2018i2p147-172.html)). Antweiler and Gulati report leakage especially in 2010–14 with the strong C$ [snippet] ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2778868)). Diesel is largely immune under IFTA ([B&K WP](https://ruor.uottawa.ca/bitstreams/5b3840ba-a78f-4bff-a863-b8111c893ebe/download)). Green notes that no study measured leakage to neighbouring jurisdictions ([Green 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/abdae9)).
- Recession and oil prices: Vancouver regular fell from 146.6 (July 2008) to 108.1 ¢/L (July 2009) ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1810000101)). Pretis includes provincial GDP, which "allows for a control of differentiated impacts across provinces during the 2008/2009 recession" ([Pretis 2022, p. 120](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10640-022-00679-w)). R&S re-estimate on a 2007–2011 subsample with "no appreciable difference" ([R&S 2014](https://uwo.scholaris.ca/items/b5e97440-77e2-48bc-9a7d-d5ffae3735c3)).
- Concurrent policies: from 2010 the per-litre rates were reduced for the biofuel mandate, and biofuels were exempt ([R&S 2012, fn 6](https://ruor.uottawa.ca/bitstreams/71b10df6-81e0-456f-b649-76fe2e78cb98/download)). This means part of the measured fossil-gasoline decline may reflect blending (my inference). L&T control for transit improvements ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/enejou/v39y2018i2p147-172.html)). There were also the SCRAP-IT program and hybrid subsidies ([R&S 2014](https://uwo.scholaris.ca/items/b5e97440-77e2-48bc-9a7d-d5ffae3735c3)), and Quebec's small carbon levy in the control group (C$3/t, under 1 ¢/L) ([B&K WP fn 9](https://ruor.uottawa.ca/bitstreams/5b3840ba-a78f-4bff-a863-b8111c893ebe/download)).
- Small donor pool: synthetic-control inference relies on at most 9 placebo provinces ([Pretis 2022, p. 134](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10640-022-00679-w)). One synthetic-control study finds emissions and gasoline *rose* relative to the control ([Arcila & Baker 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eneeco/v111y2022ics0140988322002201.html)).
- Pass-through: complete over time, per [Bernard et al. WP 1812E](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ott/wpaper/1812e.html). L&T's lower bound adjusts for incomplete pass-through ([CRDCN](https://crdcn.ca/publication/refining-the-evidence-british-columbias-carbon-tax-and-household-gasoline-consumption/)).
