**3. Evidence that preferred creditor status is real**
- **Schlegl, Trebesch and Wright (2019)**, "The Seniority Structure of Sovereign Debt", NBER Working Paper 25793, May 2019, DOI 10.3386/w25793. Quote: "We confirm that multilateral institutions such as the IMF and World Bank are senior creditors." Official bilateral debt "is junior, or at least not senior, to private sovereign debt". https://www.nber.org/papers/w25793 [O] [high] I did not check for a later journal version.
- **Cordella and Powell (2021)**, "Preferred and non-preferred creditors", Journal of International Economics 132, 103491, DOI 10.1016/j.jinteco.2021.103491. Finding: preferred lenders keep their status by lending limited volumes at low rates, not pricing by risk, and staying out of commercial restructurings. [S] [medium]

## C. India: SECI and payment security

**12. How SECI works (ICRA rating rationale, 7 July 2026; [ICRA]AAA (Stable) reaffirmed)** https://www.icra.in/Rating/ShowRationalReportFilePdfViewer/144117 [O] [high]
- **Model:** "SECI, which signs power purchase agreements (PPAs) with developers, enters into back-to-back power sales agreements (PSAs) with state distribution utilities (discoms) on similar terms". Generator agreements are signed only after the sale agreements. The 25-year term was not confirmed from this source.
- **Trading margin:** "Rs. 0.05/unit-0.07/unit". Regulator documents cite a fixed 7 paise/kWh. [S]
- **Scale:** about 66 GW has signed sale agreements at a weighted average tariff of Rs 2.74/kWh.
- **Tripartite agreement:** "signed among the Central Government, the state governments and the RBI"; SECI has been a beneficiary since October 2016; all but two states and territories have signed. Mechanism: central utilities "can request the GoI, which in turn can further instruct the RBI to debit the amount due from the state government account held with it". Last invoked in FY2021 and FY2022 (Andhra Pradesh discoms and HESCOM).
- **New and material:** agreements with certain states expire in October 2026 (others run to 2031); ICRA calls renewal "a credit monitorable".
- **Payment security fund:** funded by budget support, developer penalties and interest; covers only National Solar Mission Phase II Batches I, III and IV. About Rs 3,980 crore of liquid investments relate to it at 31 March 2026.
- **Lowest tariffs:** Rs 2.36/kWh in June 2020 (about 3.1 US cents); Rs 2.00/kWh in SECI's Rajasthan 1,070 MW auction, late 2020 (about 2.7 cents). [S] [medium]
- **Solar-plus-storage:** Rs 2.86/kWh (2 GW solar with 1 GW/4 GWh, October 2025); Rs 3.12-3.13/kWh (1,200 MW with 3,600 MWh, auction of 9 January 2026). [S] [medium]
