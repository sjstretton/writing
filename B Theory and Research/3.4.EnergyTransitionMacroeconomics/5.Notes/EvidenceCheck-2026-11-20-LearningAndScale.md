## 1. Learning rates and cost declines

**1a. Way, Ives, Mealy and Farmer (2022)** — confidence high
- Citation: Way, R., Ives, M.C., Mealy, P. and Farmer, J.D. (2022), "Empirically grounded technology forecasts and the energy transition", Joule 6(9): 2057–2082, September 2022. DOI 10.1016/j.joule.2022.08.009. Metadata from Crossref; full text opened at https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:17256b64-f822-40ef-8770-5d0fa1ddc73e/files/s8k71nj39n
- Headline finding (Summary): "Compared to continuing with a fossil fuel-based system, a rapid green energy transition will likely result in overall net savings of many trillions of dollars—even without accounting for climate damages or co-benefits of climate policy."
- Figure: "Using the 1.4% social discount rate recommended in the Stern Review, for example, the expected net present saving is roughly $12 trillion. At the higher discount rate of 5%, the expected saving is around $5 trillion." This is the Fast Transition against No Transition.
- Cost declines against fossil fuels (Introduction): "The prices of fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and gas are volatile, but after adjusting for inflation, prices now are very similar to what they were 140 years ago, and there is no obvious long-range trend. In contrast, for several decades the costs of solar photovoltaics (PV), wind, and batteries have dropped (roughly) exponentially at a rate near 10% per year. The cost of solar PV has decreased by more than three orders of magnitude since its first commercial use in 1958."
- Technology-specific learning rates from this paper: NOT FOUND in the passages I read.

**1b. IRENA, Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024** — confidence high
- Citation: IRENA (2025), Renewable power generation costs in 2024, International Renewable Energy Agency, Abu Dhabi. ISBN 978-92-9260-669-5. Opened: https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_2025.pdf
- Learning rates (Table 1.1, world): solar PV 33.8%, onshore wind 25.0%, offshore wind 15.7%. Africa: solar PV 21.9%, onshore wind 9.6%.
- These are rates for total installed cost per doubling of cumulative capacity, not for modules. Box 1.8 defines the learning rate as "the percentage reduction in TIC for each doubling of cumulative capacity".
- Quote: "Solar PV has the highest global learning rate among utility-scale technologies, at 33.8% ... Onshore wind also has a strong learning profile, with a global learning rate close to 25%."
- Solar PV, 2010–2024: LCOE "declined by 90%, from USD 0.417/kWh to USD 0.043/kWh"; total installed cost USD 691/kW, "87% lower than in 2010"; "Global crystalline silicon module costs registered a 97% decline between January 2010 and December 2024."
- Onshore wind, 2010–2024: LCOE "fell 70%, from USD 0.113/kWh to USD 0.034/kWh"; total installed cost "fell 55% ... from USD 2 324/kW to USD 1 041/kW".
- Battery storage: "Battery storage costs declined by 93% from 2010 to 2024, falling from USD 2 571/kWh to USD 192/kWh" (utility-scale BESS, total installed cost).

**1e. Increasing returns and lock-in** — confidence high
- Arthur, W.B. (1989), "Competing Technologies, Increasing Returns, and Lock-In by Historical Events", The Economic Journal 99(394): 116–131, March 1989. DOI 10.2307/2234208. Crossref plus https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v99y1989i394p116-31.html
- Abstract opening: "This paper examines the dynamics of allocation under increasing returns within a model" in which agents choose between competing technologies and each improves as it gains adoption.
- The finding that an economy can lock in to an inferior technology through early chance events came from a search summary, not from the abstract text I read.

**1f. Deployment support and learning versus price instruments** — confidence high for citations
- Grubb, M., Drummond, P., Poncia, A., McDowall, W., Popp, D., Samadi, S., Penasco, C., Gillingham, K.T., Smulders, S., Glachant, M., Hassall, G., Mizuno, E., Rubin, E.S., Dechezleprêtre, A. and Pavan, G. (2021), "Induced innovation in energy technologies and systems: a review of evidence and potential implications for CO2 mitigation", Environmental Research Letters 16(4): 043007. DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/abde07. Published 29 March 2021. Source: Crossref record and abstract.
- Finding (abstract): "Technology costs decline with cumulative investment for almost every technology studied across all time periods, when controlled for other factors. Numerous lines of evidence point to dominant causality from at-scale deployment (prior to self-sustaining diffusion) to cost reduction in this relationship." Also: "Overall innovation is cumulative, multi-faceted, and self-reinforcing in its direction (path-dependent)." The review covers over 200 papers.
- Acemoglu, D., Aghion, P., Bursztyn, L. and Hemous, D. (2012), "The Environment and Directed Technical Change", American Economic Review 102(1): 131–166, February 2012. DOI 10.1257/aer.102.1.131. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.1.131
- Finding (abstract, paraphrased by the fetch tool): with sufficiently substitutable inputs, temporary taxes or subsidies can redirect innovation to clean inputs; optimal policy combines carbon taxes with research subsidies; delay is costly. Opening sentence verbatim: "This paper introduces endogenous and directed technical change in a growth model with environmental constraints."
- Neither abstract states the exact proposition "price instruments face rising marginal abatement costs while deployment support exploits learning". Grubb et al. support the learning half. The rising-marginal-cost half: NOT FOUND.
- Stern and Stiglitz: not checked.

## 2. Interest-rate sensitivity

**Wood Mackenzie** — confidence high
- Press release, 18 April 2024, on the Horizons report "Conflicts of interest: the cost of investing in the energy transition in a high interest-rate era" (lead author named: Peter Martin). https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/2024-press-releases/renewables-and-nascent-low-carbon-technologies-most-exposed-to-high-interest-rates/
- "In the US, Wood Mackenzie analysis shows that a 2-percentage point increase in the risk-free interest rate pushes up the levelised cost of electricity (LCOE) by as much as 20% for renewables. The comparative increase in LCOE for a combined-cycle gas turbine plant is only 11%."
- Note the wording: US analysis, and "as much as" 20%.

**IRENA (2025), Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024** — confidence high (same URL as 1b)
- "While fossil fuel-based system costs tend to be dominated by variable fuel expenditures, renewable energy projects are characterised by relatively high upfront CAPEX requirements. ... The LCOE of such systems is highly dependent on the time value of project cashflows, which are driven by the cost of capital."
- "In 2024, IRENA's weighted average costs of capital (WACC) assumptions ranged from 3.8% in Europe to 12% in Africa". Onshore wind LCOE was similar in Africa (USD 0.051/kWh) and Europe (USD 0.052/kWh), but "in Africa, financing costs accounted for the majority share."

**IEA, World Energy Outlook 2025** — confidence medium (qualitative only)
- "A key issue is that the cost of capital for power generation projects in emerging market and developing economies is over twice as high as it is in advanced economies. ... A high cost of capital pushes up financing costs and makes it much more difficult to generate attractive risk-adjusted returns, especially for relatively capital-intensive investment in clean technologies."
- Read from a PDF copy of the report already in the session scratch folder; I did not record its download URL and iea.org returned 403.
- A quantified IEA statement (x-point rise in WACC raises solar or wind LCOE by y% against gas): NOT FOUND.
- IEA Renewables 2024 (same caveat on provenance): "In most of the world, WACC increased 1-2 percentage points in 2023 due to a rise in interest rates in most countries."

## 5. Import bills and the fuel-for-capital swap

**5a. Fossil fuel import bills** — confidence high for Ember quotes
- Ember, "Energy Security in an Insecure World" (Kingsmill Bond, Daan Walter, Sam Butler-Sloss), 22 April 2025. https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/energy-security-in-an-insecure-world/

**5b. Commodity exposure replaced by capital cost** — confidence high for IRENA, medium for IEA
- IRENA (2025), Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024 (URL as in 1b): "Considering the total renewable capacity currently in operation worldwide, avoided fossil fuel costs in 2024 totalled USD 467 billion (Figure 1.10).
