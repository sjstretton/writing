## 1. "The transition is an electrification of a fuel-based system"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.** The direction is right. The scale holds only in the net-zero scenario.

**(ii) Evidence**
- **Electricity share of final consumption** [P]: 21% in 2024. In 2050: Current Policies 29%, Stated Policies (STEPS) 31%, Net Zero (NZE) 55%. IEA, World Energy Outlook 2025 (12 Nov 2025), Annex Tables A.1a–c. "In the STEPS, electricity accounts for over one-quarter of total final consumption by 2035, up from 21% in 2024." NZE: "electricity increases its share in total final consumption to around 40% by 2040 and 55% by 2050." Copy read: https://www.fie.undef.edu.ar/ceptm/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/WorldEnergyOutlook2025.pdf
- **There is no APS figure**: "The WEO-2025 does not include the Announced Pledges Scenario" [P].
- **Efficiency** [P]: "electric cars are two-to-four-times more efficient than fossil fuel alternatives, and heat pumps are around three-to-five-times more efficient" (WEO 2025).
- **Waste heat** [P]: "almost two-thirds of all primary energy is wasted in energy production, transportation, and use"; 606 EJ primary in 2019, 227 EJ useful ("only 37% efficient overall"); cost "$4.6 trillion per year". RMI, "The Incredible Inefficiency of the Fossil Energy System", 4 June 2024 (Lovins, Speelman, Walter, Bond, Gulli, Butler-Sloss). https://rmi.org/the-incredible-inefficiency-of-the-fossil-energy-system/
- **Capital share** [P]: "The share of capital in total costs rises from less than 60% in 2020 (already ten percentage points higher than in 2010) to about 80% in 2050." IEA, Net Zero by 2050 (May 2021), ch. 4. This is for the electricity supply industry only. Copy: https://acee.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Net-Zero-by-2050-A-Roadmap-for-the-Global-Energy-Sector.pdf
- **Investment 2025** [P]: "capital flows to the energy sector are set to rise in 2025 to USD 3.3 trillion… Around USD 2.2 trillion is going collectively to renewables, nuclear, grids, storage, low-emissions fuels, efficiency and electrification, twice as much as the USD 1.1 trillion going to oil, natural gas and coal." IEA, World Energy Investment 2025 (June 2025). Copy: https://bdd.pseau.org/outils/ouvrages/iea_world_energy_investment_2025_2025.pdf
- **Investment 2026**: USD 3.4 trillion total; electricity supply and infrastructure USD 1.6 trillion, USD 2 trillion with end-use electrification [P] (IEA WEI 2026 launch slides, 28 May 2026, https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/de35b632-c4ae-476b-a167-97ef3b40f0fd/WorldEnergyInvestment2026_LaunchPresentation.pdf). The split of about USD 2.2 trillion clean to USD 1.2 trillion fossil (about 1.8:1) is [S] from press coverage.
- **Fuel-import bills** [P]: emerging Asia's fossil import bill rises "from USD 900 billion today to USD 1.2 trillion by 2035" under current policies; in NZE import bills are "cut by more than two-thirds by 2035" (WEO 2025). As a share of GDP: NOT FOUND.

**(iii) Caveats**
- On stated policies electricity is still under a third of final energy in 2050, so the system stays mostly fuel-based.
- Total final consumption rises in CPS and STEPS (453 EJ to 599 and 541 EJ) and falls only in NZE (352 EJ).
- The capital-share figure for the whole energy system (not just electricity) is NOT FOUND.
- RMI is an advocacy source, and some of the two-thirds loss is thermodynamically unavoidable ([S], RMI appendix).
- Fossil investment is still USD 1.1–1.2 trillion a year and reportedly rose in 2026 [S].
- Lawrence Livermore flow charts were not checked.

## 2. "Renewables are always run"

**(i) Verdict: overstated.** True as dispatch economics, false as physical or contractual fact once shares are high.

**(ii) Evidence**
- **China, official** [P]: "In 2023, curtailment rates in China were 2.0% for solar PV, 2.7 % for wind"; about 15% in the early 2010s. IEA, Renewables 2024 (Oct 2024), pp. 116–117. Copy: https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/technical/energy-and-natural-resources/renewables-2024.ashx
- **China, 2026** [P]: "8.6% of the country's solar output and 9.1% of its wind output were curtailed in the first half of 2026" (NEA). An independent GEM/CREA estimate is 26.1%, or 360 TWh, up 49% year on year. Reuters, 17 Aug 2026, republished at https://www.geo.tv/latest/677999-china-leads-wave-of-clean-power-wastage-as-grids-globally-hit-limits
- **China threshold** [P]: "China raised its wind and solar PV curtailment thresholds in 2024" (WEO 2025).
- **Chile** [P]: "In 2023, 7.7% of wind and solar PV generation was curtailed, and 2024 started with a curtailment rate of 14.5% in the first trimester" (IEA Renewables 2024). ACERA totals of 5,909 GWh (2024) and 6,084 GWh (2025) are [S].
- **California** [P]: "In 2024, CAISO curtailed 3.4 million megawatthours (MWh) of utility-scale wind and solar output", "a 29% increase" on 2023; solar was 93%. EIA, 28 May 2025, https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=65364
- **India, Australia, Japan** [P, same Reuters piece]: India "curtailed 8.13 TWh of solar power in the quarter ended June" 2026, 14% of solar output; Australia 7% and Japan 4% of renewable output in H1 2026.
- **General** [P]: "In Chile, Ireland and the United Kingdom… the curtailment of wind and solar PV recently reached between 5% and 15%" (IEA Renewables 2024).
- **Negative prices** [S]: Germany 573–576 negative-price hours in 2025; Bloomberg (5 Jan 2026) reports a European record.

**(iii) Caveats**
- The IEA treats some curtailment as efficient: it "can also serve as a cost-effective alternative to overbuilding of grid and storage infrastructure" (WEO 2025) [P].
- Take-or-pay evidence found points the other way round: the IEA cites fossil fleets "established on long-term contracts with take-or-pay clauses" as a barrier to renewables (Renewables 2024) [P].
- Deemed-generation clauses in developing-country renewable PPAs, India's must-run rules and Vietnam's 2020–21 rates: NOT FOUND.

## 3. "Long-term investment requires long-term contracts"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.** "Overwhelmingly relies on" is evidenced; "requires" is too strong.

**(ii) Evidence**
- **IEA** [P]: "Almost 84% of global renewable utility-scale capacity growth in 2024-2030 is expected to be stimulated by policy schemes"; "market-driven deployment is expected to account for 15%". Within the market-driven part, corporate PPAs are "more than 40%", green certificates similar, "unsolicited bilateral contracts with utilities (16%) and merchant projects (4%)". Renewables 2024, p. 68.
- **Derived** (my arithmetic): merchant is about 0.6% of utility-scale growth; policy schemes plus corporate and utility contracts come to about 92–93%. The quoted sentence "more than 90%… long-term revenue certainty" itself was NOT FOUND.
- **May & Neuhoff (2021)**, The Energy Journal 42(4): 131–152, DOI 10.5547/01956574.42.4.nmay [P abstract]: "between the support policies, the costs of renewable energy deployment differ by around 30 percent, but can be significantly lower or higher". This is a deployment-cost gap between policy regimes, not "CfDs cut financing cost by 30%".
- **Joskow (2022)** [P abstract]: recommends hybrid markets combining "competition for the market" through long-term PPAs with short-term markets. The journal is **Journal of Institutional Economics** 18(2): 313–329, DOI 10.1017/S1744137421000400, not Oxford Review of Economic Policy.
- **Newbery (2016)**, Energy Policy 94: 401–410, DOI 10.1016/j.enpol.2015.10.028 [P abstract]: capacity auctions "tend to over-procure capacity"; missing-market problems are neglected.
- **Roques & Finon (2017)**, Energy Policy 105: 584–596, DOI 10.1016/j.enpol.2017.02.035: citation verified, finding not verified.
- **EU reform** [S]: Regulation (EU) 2024/1747, in force 16 July 2024; direct price support for new wind, solar, geothermal, run-of-river hydro and nuclear must be two-way CfDs "or equivalent schemes" after a three-year transition. EUR-Lex returned 503, so the article text was not read.

**(iii) Caveats**
- IEA Renewables 2025 [S] has corporate PPAs, utility contracts and merchant plants at 30% of expansion to 2030, double the previous forecast; auctions are almost 60% of utility-scale additions.
- The EU rule covers publicly supported projects only.
- Long contracts move risk to the buyer or the state; see Pakistan under claim 8.

## 4. "Carbon pricing is volatile and often reversed"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.** Volatility is clear. Reversals cluster in consumer-facing fuel pricing; total coverage and revenue keep rising.

**(ii) Evidence**
- **EU ETS range**: €0.10 in Sept 2007 and a "new record low of 2.81 euros" in late Jan 2013 [P, Wikipedia, secondary]. Peak €100.34 in Feb 2023 [S, Statista]. Annual averages €83.6 (2023) and €64.8 (2024) [S]. 2025 average auction price EUR 73.43, secondary market EUR 74.35 [P, ICAP, https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/ets/eu-emissions-trading-system-eu-ets]. About €86–90 in early to mid November 2026 [S].
- **EU softening in 2026** [P]: Commission proposal of 17 July 2026 would cut the cap by 3.7% a year in 2031–35 and 1.7% in 2036–40 (against 4.3–4.4% now), halve the reserve withdrawal rate from 24% to 12% from 2028, and extend free allocation to 2038. Ten member states called the ETS an "existential risk"; Italy had called for suspension. Carbon Brief, 20 July 2026, https://www.carbonbrief.org/qa-what-the-eus-carbon-market-review-means-for-climate-action/
- **ETS2**: ICAP news item of 10 March 2026, "EU officially adopts the 2040 climate target, postpones ETS 2 by one year" [P]; start now 2028. A June 2026 safeguard releases 40m allowances if the price exceeds €45 [P, Carbon Brief].
- **Reversals, all [S]**:
  - Australia: repeal Act assented 17 July 2014, effective 1 July 2014 (legislation.gov.au).
  - Canada: fuel charge rates set to zero from 1 April 2025 (SOR/2025-107; canada.ca).
  - France: carbon component frozen at €44.60/t since 2018; the planned path to €86.20 by 2022 was dropped.
  - Washington I-2117: repeal failed, 61.95% No (Ballotpedia).
  - New Zealand: agriculture taken out of the ETS in 2024; in October 2025 the government said farm pricing would not go ahead (RNZ).
  - South Africa: phase 1 extended three years to end-2025.
  - Mexico: ETS operational phase delayed at least four times.
  - UK: OBR puts the cost of fuel duty freezes from 2010-11 to 2026-27 at £120 billion.
- **World Bank 2025** [P]: "around 28% of global greenhouse gas emissions are now covered"; revenues "exceeded $100 billion in 2024"; 80 instruments. 10 June 2025, https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/10/global-carbon-pricing-mobilizes-over-100-billion-for-public-budgets
- **World Bank 2026** [P]: "Just over 29%"; "more than $107 billion for public budgets in 2025"; 87 policies; average price "nearly $21/tCO2e". 19 May 2026, https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/05/19/direct-carbon-pricing-covers-nearly-one-third-of-global-emissions
- **Dolphin, Pollitt & Newbery**, Oxford Economic Papers, DOI 10.1093/oep/gpz042 (online 2019; volume and pages not returned by Crossref) [P abstract]: "policy stringency is a highly persistent process"; average prices remain low; mitigation "will require multiple instruments".

**(iii) Caveats**
- Counter-evidence to "often reversed": instruments rose from 80 to 87, coverage from 12% to 29% over a decade, and revenue tripled (World Bank) [P].
- Washington's repeal failed.
- Power and industry schemes tend to be diluted (EU 2026) more than abolished.
- NOT FOUND: share of emissions priced within or above the recommended range; UK ETS, California, RGGI, Korea and China price ranges; any academic count of reversals.
- Not verified: Ontario 2018 (page 403), Switzerland 2021 (page 404), Rabe (2018).

## 5. "Carbon pricing works for operational changes, weak for long-lived investment"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.** The operational effect is well evidenced; the investment half is contested.

**(ii) Evidence**
- **Leroutier (2022)**, JEEM 111: 102580, DOI 10.1016/j.jeem.2021.102580 [P abstract]: UK power emissions "declined by 20 to 26 percent per year on average between 2013 and 2017". Mechanisms include "the closure of some high-emission plants".
- **UK coal** [P]: "Coal power provided almost 40% of UK generation in 2012… shrinking to 2% by 2019"; gas rose from 100 to 143 TWh in 2015–16 as the Carbon Price Support increased. Ember, 20 Sep 2024, https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-uks-journey-to-a-coal-power-phase-out/
- **Lilliestam, Patt & Bersalli (2021)**, WIREs Climate Change 12(1): e681, DOI 10.1002/wcc.681 [P abstract]: "Some articles find short‐term operational effects, especially fuel switching in existing assets, but no article finds mentionable effects on technological change… all articles examining the effects on zero‐carbon investment found that existing carbon pricing scheme have had no effect at all."
- **van den Bergh & Savin (2021)**, Environmental and Resource Economics 80(4): 705–715, DOI 10.1007/s10640-021-00594-6 [P abstract]: the review has shortcomings; "carbon pricing has had a small but positive and significant effect on low-carbon innovation". Lilliestam et al. replied in ERE 83(3): 733–758 (2022).
- **Calel & Dechezleprêtre (2016)**, Review of Economics and Statistics 98(1): 173–191, DOI 10.1162/rest_a_00470 [P abstract]: EU ETS raised low-carbon innovation "among regulated firms by as much as 10%", "nearly a 1% increase in European low-carbon patenting".
- **Green (2021)**, Environmental Research Letters 16(4): 043004, DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/abdae9 [P abstract]: aggregate reductions "generally between 0% and 2% per year"; EU ETS 0–1.5% a year.
- **Döbbeling-Hildebrandt et al. (2024)**, Nature Communications 15: 4147, DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-48512-w [P abstract]: "-5% to -21% across the schemes (-4% to -15% after correcting for publication bias)"; 483 effect sizes, 80 evaluations, 21 schemes.
- **Rafaty, Dolphin & Pretis**: published 2025, Energy Economics 144: 108298, DOI 10.1016/j.eneco.2025.108298 (2020 was the working paper). Working-paper result [S]: emissions growth cut by 1–2.5%.

**Balance.** Fast fuel-switching where the price is high relative to the coal–gas spread is solid. Innovation effects are small but positive. Ex-post evidence that carbon prices drove zero-carbon investment is essentially absent.

**(iii) Caveats**
- Absence of investment evidence comes from low-price regimes with overlapping renewable support, so it does not show pricing cannot work.
- Leroutier's plant closures are an effect on long-lived capital, on the exit side.
- Not verified: Wilson & Staffell (2018), Nature Energy 3(5): 365–372, DOI 10.1038/s41560-018-0109-0 (citation only); EU 2019–20 and German coal-to-gas switching; Dixit & Pindyck.

## 6. "Investment takes a long time"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.** True for grids, nuclear and currently turbines and transformers; not for solar, batteries or chargers.

**(ii) Evidence**
- **Grids** [P]: "New grid infrastructure often takes five to 15 years to plan, permit and complete, compared with one to five years for new renewables projects and less than two years for new EV charging infrastructure"; an extra-high-voltage line takes "5 to 13 years" in advanced economies. IEA, Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions (Oct 2023), https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/ea2ff609-8180-4312-8de9-494bcf21696d/ElectricityGridsandSecureEnergyTransitions.pdf. The IEA figure is 5–15 years, not 10–15.
- **Queues** [P]: "At least 3 000 gigawatts (GW) of renewable power projects, of which 1 500 GW are in advanced stages" (same 2023 report; both numbers, not a later revision). WEO 2025: "some 2 800 GW of solar and wind projects, more than half of them at an advanced stage"; elsewhere "Around 1 700 GW… at advanced stages".
- **Nuclear** [P]: "The mean construction time for all seven reactors was 114 months" for 2024 grid connections, from 61 months (Zhangzhou 1) to 204 (Flamanville 3). The median is 103 months (my calculation from the report's table). World Nuclear Association, page updated 1 Sep 2025, https://world-nuclear.org/our-association/publications/world-nuclear-performance-report/Global-nuclear-industry-performance. WEO 2025 [P]: large reactors in Europe and the US "on average have been completed eight years later than planned and cost 2.5-times as much".
- **Gas turbines** [P]: GE Vernova backlog plus slot reservations 116 GW at end Q2 2026; "taking reservations for 2031 deliveries now"; books "four to five years out". Utility Dive, 23 July 2026, https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-climbs-to-116-gw/826039/. The 5–7 year figure was NOT FOUND.
- **Transformers**: 30% supply deficit for power transformers and 10% for distribution in 2025 [P, Wood Mackenzie, 14 Aug 2025, https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/power-transformers-and-distribution-transformers-will-face-supply-deficits-of-30-and-10-in-2025]. Lead times of 128 weeks (power) and 144 weeks (generator step-up) in Q2 2025 are [S]. WEO 2025 [P]: "Lead times for large power transformers and high-voltage cables have nearly doubled in many regions and wait times for HVDC systems stretch into the 2030s."
- **Asset lives** [P]: grid equipment "lifetime of 30 to 40 years"; grids "around 40 years" (IEA grids report).

**(iii) Caveats**
- "Significantly shorter lead times for transmission lines are observed in China and India" [P]; China builds reactors in about five years.
- Much of the delay is permitting and queueing, which is a policy variable.
- The IEA calls the turbine price spike "less likely to indicate long-term implications" [P].
- NOT FOUND: offshore wind and utility solar lead times from a primary source; generation asset lives of 25–60 years.

## 7. "Fuel price rises are unpopular"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.**

**(ii) Evidence**
- **McCulloch, Natalini, Hossain & Justino (2022)**, World Development 157: 105935, DOI 10.1016/j.worlddev.2022.105935 [P abstract]: "Between 2005 and 2018, 41 countries had at least one riot directly associated with popular demand for fuel"; large price shocks are the driver; fixed-price regimes produce large adjustments, "often leading to riots".
- **Ecuador** [P, Wikipedia]: Decree 883 took effect 3 Oct 2019 (diesel roughly doubled); withdrawn 13 Oct; Decree 894 restored subsidies 14 Oct; 8 deaths.
- **Kazakhstan** [P, Wikipedia]: LPG cap lifted 1 Jan 2022, price to about 120 tenge a litre; cap of 50 tenge restored for six months; 227 killed per officials.
- **Nigeria** [S]: 2012, N65 to N141, cut back to N97 after strikes; 2023, about N190 to about N700.
- **Angola** [S]: July 2025, diesel 300 to 400 kwanza; 22 deaths per government, later reports 30.
- **France**: see claim 4.
- **IMF 2023 update**, WP/23/169, 24 Aug 2023, Black, Liu & Parry [P]: "$7 trillion" in 2022, "7.1 percent of GDP"; explicit share 18% (about US$1.3 trillion by arithmetic; the dollar figure is not on the page). https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2023/08/22/IMF-Fossil-Fuel-Subsidies-Data-2023-Update-537281
- **IMF 2025 update**, WP/25/270, 19 Dec 2025, Black, Celniak, Garcia Huitron, Parry, Schulz Antipa & Vernon-Lin [P]: "Explicit (or fiscal) subsidies were $725 billion (0.6 percent of GDP) in 2024"; implicit "$6.7 trillion (5.8 percent of GDP)". https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/12/20/underpriced-and-overused-fossil-fuel-subsidies-data-2025-update-572729
- **Douenne & Fabre (2022)**, AEJ: Economic Policy 14(1): 81–110, DOI 10.1257/pol.20200092 [P abstract]: after the Yellow Vests the French "would largely reject a tax and dividend policy"; they "overestimate their net monetary losses".
- **Dechezleprêtre, Fabre, Kruse, Planterose, Sanchez Chico & Stantcheva (2025)**, AER 115(4): 1258–1300, DOI 10.1257/aer.20230501 [P abstract]: 40,000 respondents in 20 countries; support depends on perceived effectiveness, impact on low-income households, and self-interest.

**(iii) Caveats**
- The evidence concerns large, sudden adjustments from fixed-price regimes; opposition is partly belief-driven and depends on design and revenue use.
- Nigeria's 2023 removal was not reversed [S].
- Not verified: Iran 2019, Haiti 2018, Sudan 2018–19, Indonesia, Kenya 2023–24, Nigeria's August 2024 protests.

## 8. "Energy costs and risks are macro-critical"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.**

**(ii) Evidence**
- **Global bill** [P]: "Total costs of energy to all consumers today are around USD 9 trillion each year, or about 10% of global GDP" (WEO 2025). The 13% figure for 2022 was NOT FOUND; the IEA's 2022 bill "likely to top USD 10 trillion" is [S].
- **Europe's crisis support** [P]: "€651 billion has been allocated and earmarked across European countries" since Sept 2021: "€540 billion in the EU, of which €158 billion… Germany", "€103 billion in the UK", "€8.1 billion in Norway". Bruegel dataset, last update 26 June 2023, when totals were revised down. https://www.bruegel.org/dataset/national-policies-shield-consumers-rising-energy-prices
- **Import bills** [P]: emerging Asia USD 900 billion today, USD 1.2 trillion by 2035 under current policies (WEO 2025).
- **Pakistan, all [S]**: circular debt about PKR 2.4 trillion (2.1% of GDP) in mid-2024, about PKR 1.6 trillion in June 2025 and PKR 1.835 trillion at end FY2025-26 (IMF target missed); capacity payments about Rs 2.1 trillion in FY24 and Rs 1.8 trillion in FY25; system utilisation about one-third; contracts being renegotiated from take-or-pay to take-and-pay.
- **2026 shock** [P]: "the Middle East conflict is reinforcing a strong prioritisation of energy security in country investment strategies" (IEA WEI 2026 launch slides).

**(iii) Caveats**
- The 10% is gross spending, not a loss; Bruegel's figure is earmarked, not spent, and its top-of-range vintage was not verified.
- Pakistan is evidence that long-term contracts (claim 3) create contingent liabilities.
- Not verified: Trimble, Kojima, Perez Arroyo & Mohammadzadeh (2016), World Bank Policy Research WP 7788, DOI 10.1596/1813-9450-7788 (citation verified; the 1.5% of GDP figure not seen, page 403). Ghana's excess-capacity charges and South Asia estimates: NOT FOUND.

## 9. "Renewables are cheap but storage roughly doubles the cost"

**(i) Verdict: overstated for evening shifting; roughly fair for near-24-hour supply.**

**(ii) Evidence**
- **Solar and wind alone** [P]: solar PV "USD 0.043/kWh", onshore wind "USD 0.034/per kilowatt hour"; "91% of newly commissioned utility-scale capacity" cheaper than the cheapest new fossil alternative; solar 41% and wind 53% cheaper. IRENA, Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024 (July 2025), https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_Summary_2025.pdf
- **Batteries**: installed cost fell "from USD 2 571/kWh to USD 192/kWh" between 2010 and 2024 [P, IRENA]. BNEF December 2025 figures are [S]: packs $108/kWh, stationary packs $70/kWh, turnkey systems $117/kWh (China $73, Europe $177, US $219), down 31% in a year.
- **Shifting half of output** [P]: all-in battery "$125/KWh as of October 2025" outside China and the US; levelised cost of storage "$65/MWh"; storage "adds about 33 $/MWh to the total cost of solar", giving "$76/MWh" against $43/MWh. That is +77%. Ember, 11 Dec 2025, https://ember-energy.org/latest-updates/batteries-now-cheap-enough-to-deliver-solar-when-it-is-needed/
- **Near-24-hour, Ember** [P]: "just $104/MWh to get 97% of the way to 24/365 solar, 22% lower cost than just a year earlier" (Las Vegas; 5 kW solar plus 17 kWh battery per constant kW; coal $118, nuclear $182). Ember, 21 June 2025, https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/solar-electricity-every-hour-of-every-day-is-here-and-it-changes-everything/
- **Near-24-hour, IEA** [P]: 100 MW firm at 95% capacity factor needs "600 MW of solar PV and 400 MW/1 600 MWh of battery storage", at "around USD 75/MWh in China, USD 135/MWh in the United States and USD 180/MWh in the European Union" (WEO 2025, p. 202).
- **India, solar plus four-hour storage** [P]: "record low INR 2.86 tariff" ($0.033/kWh) for 2 GW solar with 1 GW/4 GWh. pv magazine, 16 Oct 2025, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/10/16/indias-solar-plus-storage-tender-sets-record-low-inr-2-86-tariff/. Standalone solar at about INR 2.55–2.60 is [S].
- **India, round-the-clock** [P]: "INR 5.25 ($0.055)/kWh" with a 90% annual demand-fulfilment requirement. pv magazine, 10 Aug 2026, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/10/secis-1-gw-fdre-rtc-power-tender-discovers-inr-5-25-kwh-tariff/
- **US hybrids** [P, IRENA]: 17 operating projects averaged "USD 0.079/kWh", against gas combined-cycle USD 0.077 and coal USD 0.119. Lazard's 2025 US solar range is $38–78/MWh, average 58 [P, PV Tech, 17 June 2025, https://www.pv-tech.org/us-utility-scale-solar-pv-lcoe-tightens-to-us38-78-mwh-in-2025-lazard/].

**Conclusion (my arithmetic on the figures above)**
- **(a) Evening shifting, about four hours:** adds roughly 10–15% in Indian auctions where only part of output is shifted, and up to about +$33/MWh (+75–80%) when half of output is shifted at global-average costs. "Roughly doubles" is too high.
- **(b) Near-24-hour supply:** about 2 to 2.4 times solar-only (India round-the-clock about 2.0×; Ember $104 against $43 about 2.4×). "Roughly doubles" is fair in sunny, low-cost markets and an understatement elsewhere (IEA: $135 US, $180 EU). Costs are falling about 20–30% a year.

**(iii) Caveats**
- The Ember $104 is one sunny city compared with a global-average solar cost; cloudier sites reach far less (Birmingham 62%) [P].
- IRENA notes system integration costs of USD 0.028–0.032/kWh at 60–90% variable shares (CSIRO modelling) [P], and cost of capital of 3.8% in Europe against 12% in Africa [P], which weighs on capital-heavy storage.
- NOT FOUND: Lazard 2025 solar-plus-storage range and firming cost (PDF 404; CAISO "about $43 to over $70" is [S]); UAE project tariff (only cost of $5.9–6.1 billion, [S]); Chile, Saudi and Morocco tariffs; IEA India solar-plus-battery versus coal.

## 10. "The transition is cross-sectoral; investors must coordinate expectations"

## 12. "Fossil fuel consumption is largely fungible"

**(i) Verdict: supported with caveats.** Strongest for oil supply cuts; "largely" overstates it for trade in energy-intensive goods.

**(ii) Evidence**
- **Sinn (2008)**, International Tax and Public Finance 15(4): 360–394, DOI 10.1007/s10797-008-9082-z [P abstract]: demand cuts by some countries "simply depress the world price of carbon and induce the environmental sinners to consume what the Kyoto countries have economized on"; suppliers may "extract their stocks more rapidly".
- **Harstad (2012)**, Journal of Political Economy 120(1): 77–115, DOI 10.1086/665405 [P abstract]: "If a climate coalition reduces its emissions, world prices change and nonparticipants typically emit more"; best policy is "to simply buy foreign deposits and conserve them".
- **Prest (2022)**, JAERE 9(4): 681–720, DOI 10.1086/718963 [P abstract]: after leakage a US federal leasing ban cuts emissions by "about one-quarter of the amount originally projected for the Clean Power Plan". The leakage rate of 50–75% is from the 2020 working paper and testimony [S], so about 50% is the low end.
- **Fæhn, Hagem, Lindholt, Mæland & Rosendahl (2017)**, The Energy Journal 38(1): 77–102, DOI 10.5547/01956574.38.1.tfae [P abstract]: for Norway, "about 2/3 of the emission reductions should come through supply side measures".
- **IPCC AR6 WGIII ch. 13 (2022)** [P]: "There is no consistent evidence of significant emissions leakage or competitiveness effects between countries, including for emissions-intensive trade-exposed industries covered by emission trading systems (medium evidence, medium agreement)." Ex-ante models "typically estimate significant leakage"; ex-post studies find "no or insignificant leakage". https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_Chapter_13.pdf

**(iii) Caveats**
- Every estimate found implies leakage below 100%, so unilateral cuts still reduce global emissions.
- The IPCC's ex-post reading contradicts large industrial leakage.
- NOT FOUND: the 5–30% IPCC range in the chapter text searched; the Erickson & Lazarus 0.6-barrel figure (Nature Climate Change 4(9): 778–781, DOI 10.1038/nclimate2335; citation verified, pages blocked).
