## 3. Proposal review

**Length and structure.** The main text runs from the title to the end of the Methods section and is about **2,460 words** by extraction (the document states 2,445). That fits the usual length. It covers background, research questions, framework, methods, ethics, feasibility, supervisor and timeline, and is competently written.

### Must fix before anyone at Oxford sees it

1. **Drafting artefacts.** Three passages read as tool or planning text:
   - "Prepared from the supplied research, analytical and planning documents; broader and contradictory strands are preserved in the appendices."
   - Appendix D: "because of the **user's** stated agreement".
   - The "Planning assumption" header line.

   Remove all three.
2. **Appendices B-E** describe "contradictory" source notes, parked ideas and claims that de-risking is "nearly free". They signal an unfocused applicant. Submit the main text and bibliography only, plus at most a one-page timeline (Appendix A).
3. **Supervisor claim.** "Dr Gireesh Shrimali has agreed to act as the proposed primary supervisor" needs written confirmation from him before you submit.
   - Search results confirm he is Head of Transition Finance Research, Oxford Sustainable Finance Group ([OSF page](https://sustainablefinance.ox.ac.uk/?p=4724)).
   - It is **unverified** whether a research-track post can act as sole primary DPhil supervisor. Ask him, and name a co-supervisor.

### Substantive weaknesses (frank)

- **Scope is too large for 3 years.** The plan has 4 work packages: project finance, real options, a political-economy module, a CPAT link and 2-3 case studies with 20-30 interviews. Assessors will doubt feasibility. Make WP2-3 the core and treat WP4 as validation.
- **Identification and data are vague.** RQ1 and RQ2 are empirical, but the proposal names no dataset for cost of capital, guarantee pricing or recoveries. Name the sources, for example:
  - the Oxford "Energy Transition Risk and Cost of Capital" programme ([report](https://sustainablefinance.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-energy-transition-and-changing-financing-costs.pdf)), which is also your best fit with the supervisor's group;
  - Steffen (2020);
  - public MDB guarantee documents.

  State what is estimated and what is simulated.
- **The central hypothesis is hard to falsify.** "Most effective when it complements rather than substitutes" needs a testable form. One example: a threshold condition under which a given WACC reduction changes the sovereign's adoption decision.
- **The political-economy module is underspecified.** "A parsimonious model of policy resistance" is a phrase, not a model. Give one sentence on the formal structure and on how it will be calibrated.
- **The literature base is thin (13 items)** for a proposal whose claimed contribution is integrating the literatures. Missing:
  - the MDB capital-adequacy and guarantee-pricing literature, including the G20 independent review of MDB capital adequacy frameworks (2022);
  - conditionality and results-based finance;
  - sovereign-risk and cost-of-capital work after 2020.

  Verify every addition; do not add citations from memory.
- **Novelty against UNDP DREI.** DREI already compares the costs of public instruments. Say in one sentence what this project adds: the sovereign incentive and dynamic credibility.
- **Fit with the department.** SoGE is interdisciplinary. Add one or two sentences on the spatial and institutional dimension (country variation in risk) and how it ties to the Smith School's research.
- **Full-time is assumed but not explained.** You are at the World Bank. Assessors will ask whether you will leave or go part-time. Decide, and make the timeline consistent with that decision.
