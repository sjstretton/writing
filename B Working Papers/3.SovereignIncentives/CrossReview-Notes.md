# Cross-review notes for series 2 (Finance and Risk) and series 3 (Sovereign Incentives)

Cross-review of the ten documents (brief and working paper for 2.1, 2.2, 3.1, 3.2, 3.3), 29 October 2026. Each item gives the issue found and how it was resolved. Open items for Stephen are at the end. The 2.0 and 3.0 overviews, `PAPERS.md`, `TODO.md`, `research.qmd` and the website were not edited here.

## Resolved

1. **Spelling and titles in the bibliographies.** Seven of the ten `.bib` files gave 3.1's title as "Notional Capitalised Value". Every `CFPB2x`, `CFPB3x` and `StrettonWP2x`/`3x` entry in all ten bibs now uses the final title from each paper's YAML, including "Reward the Stock as Well as the Flow: Notional Capitalized Value and Guarantee-Based Climate Incentives" and "From Projects to Asset Classes: Two-Stage Finance and Synthetic Sovereign Bonds for Clean Power". Entries are now in one form, one line each, with notes such as "Working paper 3.1".
2. **Stale series 1 title.** The 3.2 bibs gave `CFPB12` as "Feebates: Pricing Carbon Without Raising Average Costs". It now matches the 1.2 brief: "Feebates: The Full Carbon Price at the Margin, Without the Carbon Bill".
3. **Wrong book subtitle in 2.2.** Both 2.2 bibs called the book manuscript *Time and Chance: Risk, Guarantees and the Cost of Clean Power in Developing Countries*. They now use the subtitle in the book master (`6.Additional/6.Books/6.1.TimeAndChance/TimeAndChance.qmd`): *The Elements of Scale in the Green Transition*. See open item 3.
4. **US spelling in the text.** Fixed: "Standardised" (2.0 title in the 2.2 brief's related list), "modelled", "centres", "cheque", "labelled" (3.1, 3.3), "channelling" and "rechannelling" (3.2), "cancelling" (3.1), "towards" (2.1) and "instalments" (2.1). Proper names keep their own spelling (United Nations Development Programme). Both `SeriesArchitecture.md` files now use US spelling for shared terms ("notional capitalized value", "standardization", "securitization", "stylized"). Folder and file names were not renamed.
5. **Guarantee loss parameters (3.3 against 3.1 and 3.2).** 3.3 used a 1 percent annual default probability, 40 percent loss given default and a 10 percent buffer costing 2 percent a year, which gave a ratio of about 5. 3.1 and 3.2 use 3 percent annual calls, correlation 0.2, 37 percent loss per call (Cruces and Trebesch 2013) and a 9.3 percent buffer held in safe assets, which gives 2.7 (10 at a 10 percent loss). **3.3 is now aligned.** `2.Source/model.py` takes the series base case as named constants and prints the sensitivities. Both figures were redrawn: Figure 2 now plots the call probability from 0.5 to 5 percent at a 37 percent loss and marks the base case. The text is updated in the abstract, contribution (4), Table 3 and its note, Section 5.2, the Figure 2 caption, Section 7.1, Section 8, Appendix B (the threshold is now 189 bp, not 260) and Appendix C, and in the brief's executive summary, Figure 1 caption and Section 6.
   - New 3.3 numbers: \$3.3 billion of capacity is worth \$772 million to the government and costs the funder \$286 million in expected loss (was \$154 million), with a \$310 million buffer held. The ratio is 2.7 (was 5.0), 10 at a 10 percent loss per call, and 2.3 with a 2 percent buffer carry. At 300 bp the leverage runs from about 16 (0.5 percent calls) to 1.6 (5 percent). At 150 bp it falls below 1 above about 4 percent.
   - 3.1's abstract, conclusion and brief abstract now say "about 2.7 dollars", not "about three", so all three papers quote the same multiplier: 2.7, 10 at a 10 percent loss, about 80 on the MDB record.
   - The 2.1 ratio of 5.3 (tariff saving per dollar of expected cost for a contract guarantee on utility arrears) is a different quantity, on different parameters. It is not inconsistent with the sovereign base case, but readers comparing the series should be told so (open item 6).
6. **LCOE basis (2.1 against 2.2).** It is the same stylized plant in both papers. 2.1's Table 1 gives 5.72 c/kWh at 10 percent with capital spent at commissioning. 2.2's worked example gives 6.2 c/kWh because it adds two years of construction interest. All four table notes now say which basis they use and point to the other paper (2.1 brief Table 1; 2.1 WP Table 1; 2.2 brief Tables 1 and 3; 2.2 WP Tables 1 and 4).
7. **"Adds nothing to liabilities" wording.** 3.2 WP Section 3 said the arrangement "adds to the government's liabilities ... which is smaller, contingent and contracted, not a new debt", which garbled the series wording. It now reads: the arrangement does not leave the government's liabilities untouched; its exposure becomes smaller, contingent and contracted. No other paper claims the structures are free to the host. 2.1 quotes the World Bank Group's "no negative impact" description of the Azura put option in order to criticize it; that is kept.
8. **SECI and the RBI.** No paper says SECI has credit lines with the RBI. 2.1 (brief and WP) describes the tripartite agreement under which the RBI debits a defaulting state's account (ICRA 2025; Kenning 2017), and the WP says explicitly that older drafts were wrong. 2.2 mentions SECI only as the payment intermediary. No change was needed.
9. **Uruguay and Thailand facts.** 3.1 and 3.2 now agree with 3.3: a \$350 million loan in 2023, cap about \$12.5 million, step-down of up to 100 bp a year; the 2022 bond moves ±15 bp per KPI with two KPIs; Thailand's bond was 2024. 3.2 WP Section 8 said "a step-down of a few basis points"; it now says "up to 100 basis points ... as in Uruguay's 2023 World Bank loan". The series 3 plan said "Thailand 2022" in the 3.3 brief outline; that is corrected to 2024.
10. **TFFF facts (3.1 against 3.2).** The facts already agreed on about \$4 per hectare, \$25 billion junior and \$100 billion senior, the World Bank as trustee, the 0.5 percent eligibility threshold and \$6.7 billion pledged. 3.2 lacked the deduction rule, and its "20 percent" for Indigenous peoples differed from 3.1's "at least 20 percent". Both 3.2 documents now state the 100-to-200-times deduction (citing Mongabay 2025, as 3.1 does) and "at least 20 percent". The official concept note was not cheaply findable online; see open item 1.
11. **3.2 and 3.1's design for surplus balance.** 3.2 (WP Section 3 and brief Section 3) now says, briefly, that NCV balance above a country's borrowing need flows to its beneficiary account, and that a country that borrows little can receive interest on its balance there instead.
12. **Related papers lists.** All five briefs now list siblings by number with full final titles. Missing links were added: 2.1 now lists 3.1 (guarantee capacity can stand behind its waterfall); 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3 list the 3.0 overview. A missing `CFPB31` entry in the 2.1 brief bib was added. No paper calls the CIT 2.2 or SLL 3.2. The 3.2 lineage appendix mentions the "mechanism paper" history only as history.
13. **Missing bib entries.** `CrucesTrebesch2013` and `GEMs2025` (3.3), `Mongabay2025` (3.2) and `CFPB31` (2.1 brief) were added. All ten documents render with no citeproc warnings.
14. **Smaller consistency fixes.** The 2.1 brief's Table 2 note said "a third" of offtaker losses fall on lenders, against 30 percent (θ = 0.3) in the WP; it now says 30 percent.
15. **Housekeeping.** `2.2.../3.RelatedPiecesAndLinks/StandardizedTwoStage/README.md` pointed to "2.4 Equity De-risking" and "section 3.4 of 2.1". ## Open items for Stephen

1. **TFFF concept note.** The deduction figures (100 times the hectare rate below 0.3 percent deforestation, 200 times at 0.3–0.5 percent, fire-degraded forest at 35 hectares) still come from Mongabay's report of the concept note. Replace the source with the official TFFF concept note before formal publication. 3.1 Appendix B already says so.
2. **Is the 3 percent / 37 percent base case too conservative?** It is about 30 times the MDB sovereign loss record. All three papers now present it as conservative, with 10 percent loss and the MDB record as alternatives. Decide whether the headline should stay at 2.7.
3. **Book subtitle.** The book master still says *The Elements of Scale in the Green Transition*, but the peer-review response says that phrase was a chapter title that was renamed. Confirm the subtitle; it is cited in every bib of both series.
4. **Brief and working-paper versions.** The 2.1, 3.1 and 3.2 briefs carry `version: "1.0"`, while their working papers are 0.1–0.2. Confirm that is intended.
5. **3.3 citation key.** 3.3 cites the book as `StrettonTimeAndChance`; other papers use `StrettonTC` (2.2 uses `TimeAndChance`). Every key resolves within its own file, so nothing renders wrongly, but one key across the series would be tidier.
6. **Two "five-to-one" ratios.** 2.1's contract guarantee saves \$5.3 per dollar of expected cost. That is a different ratio from the sovereign incentive multiplier (2.7). The overview should not present them as the same number.
7. **2.2 subordination range.** The WP abstract says "5 to 10 percent" subordination "at plausible correlations" (0.10–0.30). The brief and the WP introduction say "5 to 8 percent at moderate correlation" (0.10–0.20). Both are right; pick one for the overview.
8. **Legacy briefings** (`3.2.../1.Presentation/Briefing-CIT.qmd`, `Briefing-TechnicalSummaryCIT.qmd`, `2.1.../1.Presentation/Briefing-REPaymentIntermediaryFacility.qmd`) still contain old wording and numbers.
