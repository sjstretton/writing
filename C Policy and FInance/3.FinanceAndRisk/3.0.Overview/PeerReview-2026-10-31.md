# Peer review of the section 3 overview, v0.2, and its theme deck (31 Oct 2026)

Written before revision, after papers 3.1 and 3.2 were peer-reviewed and revised; it checks the overview against them.

## Summary judgement

A strong overview: the risk-stack table (each risk, its cheapest holder, its instrument) is the section's best page, and the account of how the pieces compose is clear. But both papers' headline numbers have since been corrected by their peer reviews, so the overview now overstates both.

## Major points

1. **3.1's headline is wrong here.** "Saves about five dollars for each dollar of the guarantor's expected cost" (twice) came from v0.2, which counted payments by the waterfall layers as recovery while counting them as costs elsewhere. On one basis a contract guarantee saves about \$2 per dollar of expected public cost (\$0.94 million against \$0.48 million a year in the stylized case), \$3.6 if the facility recovers 80 percent of arrears; a debt guarantee, compared on offtaker risk only, saves \$1.4 per dollar. "Guaranteeing debt saves less than it costs" no longer holds as stated. The gain is real only if the facility collects better or makes default rarer; otherwise the guarantee moves risk to the state. Say so.
2. **3.2's headline is wrong here.** "Cuts the levelized cost of solar power by about a quarter" came from refinancing the whole plant at the pool rate; only the senior debt (80 percent) is refinanced, so the saving is about a fifth (19 percent), and 14 percent with the thicker cushions real deals carry. The cushion is 5–8 percent in a large pool (not 5–10), and pooling across ten countries cuts it from about 9 to about 5 percent, about 40 percent less.
3. **The cost-of-capital source.** "Two to three times higher" cites the IEA's 2022 work, which neither paper could read; both now cite IEA (2024), "well over twice". Align.
