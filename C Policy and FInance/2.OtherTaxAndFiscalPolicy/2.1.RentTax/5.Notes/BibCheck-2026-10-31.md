# Reference check for 2.1, working paper v0.2 and brief v1.0 (31 Oct 2026)

Version 0.1 had no bibliography: its sources were named in a "Notes on further reading" appendix, and its factual claims were uncited. For v0.2 every source cited in either form was found and checked against the source itself, or, where the full text was closed, against the abstract plus a reliable summary; bibliographic details were checked against Crossref, RePEc/IDEAS, publisher pages, the Court of Justice, the OECD and Eurostat (data downloaded through their APIs). Each entry's `note` field in `../RentTax.bib` records what was read. The brief's `.bib` is copied from the working paper's, so the two cannot drift apart. No personal data was sent in any request.

**Totals.** 55 entries in `RentTax.bib` (53 cited in the working paper, 26 in the brief, including the series entries). All were checked; 9 claims carried over from v0.1 were re-worded or cut (table below); 50 sources are new.

## Claims from v0.1 that changed

| Source or claim in v0.1 | Problem found | Change made |

| "Belgium ran a version from 2006 to 2018; Italy from 2011 to 2023" | Belgium's notional interest deduction was narrowed in 2018 and abolished for periods ending on or after 31 Dec 2023 (Program Law of 26 Dec 2022); Italy's ACE was repealed from 2024 by Legislative Decree 216/2023 | Dates corrected and cited |

- Feldstein (1977), Demsetz (1973), Garnaut and Clunies Ross (1975), Weyl and Fabinger (2013): metadata plus the argument as stated in summaries and later literature; each is cited only for its well-known central argument.
- Dwyer (2014): metadata and an abstract fragment; cited only for the history of the idea.
- *Commission v Poland* and *National Grid Indus*: practitioner summaries (KPMG; PwC); EUR-Lex returned empty pages from this session.
- Brown (1948): cited from the reference list of Auerbach et al. (2017); page range not confirmed, so omitted.
