# Peer review of the section 1 overview, v0.2, and its theme deck (31 Oct 2026)

Review written before revision, as for the papers. The overview summarizes 1.1–1.4 and 6.1, so it must be revised after those papers; points that depend on their revised numbers are marked (after papers).

## Summary judgement

A clear, well-ordered overview: the one-instrument-two-jobs diagnosis, a table assigning each job to an instrument, a section per paper, three results and "What to do". It is out of date with paper 1.1 v0.4 and reads as a summary of summaries in places. The theme deck is short and on the old format.

## Major points

1. **Numbers from 1.1 are the old stylized ones.** "\$15 per tonne where a tax ... raises it by \$85" comes from the retired stylized example; 1.1 now uses the EU default values for steel: at \$80/tCO₂ a tax raises the producer price by 25 percent for a mill at Türkiye's default intensity, a feebate by 8 percent (about a third as much). Replace in the abstract, the feebate section and Table 2.
2. **The race-to-the-top condition is stated as in 1.1 v0.3** ("at verified or default intensities"). 1.1 v0.4 restates it: the result holds when importers charge by exporting country and sector, because no single plant can move that charge; with plant-level charges firms respond on their own, and the feebate extends the incentive to all output. Restate it, and link it to 1.4's country-sector proposal, which is the same measurement.
3. **The headline numbers of 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4 must match their revised papers** (after papers): the India tariff effect (\$1 against \$36/MWh), the 0.41 kg CO₂e per euro basket, the 40 percent share of consumption emissions outside the EU, the €100–600 shuffling saving.
4. **Facts to check against the sources now cited in 1.1**: carbon pricing coverage (about 28 percent of global emissions; about \$19 per tonne where a price applies, \$5 averaged over all emissions); CBAM Article 9 as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 (a price "effectively paid", net of any rebate: the textual reason a feebate earns no credit); the free-allocation phase-out (2026–2034 under Directive (EU) 2023/959; a July 2026 proposal would extend it to 2038).
