# Peer review of the section 4 overview, v0.2, and its theme deck (31 Oct 2026)

Written before revision, after papers 4.1–4.3 were peer-reviewed and revised; it checks the overview against them.

## Summary judgement

A clear overview with one strong idea, that the international system should pay in balance-sheet strength rather than cash, and a good comparison table of the four routes. The papers' reviews have since qualified several of its numbers, and the overview now states them without the conditions the papers found.

## Major points

1. **The 80-to-1 headline needs its two qualifications** (4.1, 4.2). It is a budget ratio: a borrower that counter-indemnifies a development bank gives up its option to default, so its economic gain net of that option is about 27 per dollar on the record (1.9 in the stress case). And the ratio assumes zero carry and an indefinite horizon: at a 2 percent carry it is 63 on the record and 2.3 in the stress case, and a facility that prices on the record while holding the stress buffer delivers about 13, not 80.
2. **"\$5 billion could support about \$1 trillion" ignores liquidity** (4.2). A call needs cash before recoveries arrive; if each call costs a year's debt service, \$5 billion on the record supports about \$390 billion, unless a liquidity line from the highly rated backer is added.
3. **The Trust's growth** (4.2): "grows without new donations only if it charges fees" is out of date. Holding the stress buffer, it grows about 3 percent a year with no fee if losses follow the record and shrinks about 8.5 percent a year at stress losses; with \$95 billion of private co-investment (an upper case) it reaches about \$34 billion after 10 years.
4. **4.1's \$30 per tonne** holds only if capacity binds and the scheme has no end date; with a 2050 end, a tonne emitted in 2027 costs about \$20 (4.1).
5. **The cash comparison** uses 3.7 billion tonnes (\$370 billion); 4.2 now uses the Global Carbon Budget 2025: a tenth of 37.8 billion tonnes, about \$380 billion a year at \$100 per tonne.
6. **4.3** should carry its main correction: a market sustainability-linked bond buys commitment, not money, and a government issues one only if the label premium covers the policy's cost, which is why targets are easy.
