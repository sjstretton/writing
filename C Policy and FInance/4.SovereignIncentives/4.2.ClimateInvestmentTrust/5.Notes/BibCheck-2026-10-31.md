# Reference check for 4.2, working paper v0.3 and brief v1.0 (31 Oct 2026)

Every source cited in either form was checked against the source itself (abstract at minimum; the passage where the claim is a number, a date or a term), and bibliographic details against Crossref, NBER, IDEAS/RePEc, the IMF, the EIB, the World Bank and the publishers' pages. Each entry's `note` field in `../ClimateInvestmentTrust.bib` records what was read. The brief's `.bib` is copied from the working paper's, for exactly the keys the brief cites, plus `StrettonWP42`. The chapter citations to the author's two book manuscripts were checked against the manuscripts in the repo. No personal data was sent in any request.

## New sources added (all verified)

- Schlegl, Trebesch and Wright (2019), NBER WP 25793: the IMF and World Bank are senior creditors in practice. The closest evidence for the base case.
- Perraudin, Powell and Yang (2016), IDB WP 697: rating agency methods give too little allowance for preferred creditor status.
- Friedlingstein et al. (2026), *Global Carbon Budget 2025*, ESSD 18: 3211–3288: fossil CO₂ in 2024 and China's share.
- World Bank (2025), International Debt Report press release: external debt of low- and middle-income countries \$8.9 trillion in 2024 (the benchmark that shows v0.2's growth calibration implied an impossible exposure).
- FMO (2019), Climate Investor One final close at \$850 million: the closest working vehicle to the Trust's investment function.
- Attridge and Engen (2019), ODI; IEA (2024), *Reducing the Cost of Capital*: entries and verification taken from paper 3.2's `.bib`, where both were checked the same day; the claims used here are the ones verified there.
- Reuters (2026) on the TFFF, as above.
